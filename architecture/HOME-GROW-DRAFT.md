## Growing at home: what happens to the harvest?

**What the law says.** Adults 21+ may grow up to 6 plants each, and no more than 12 at a single residence, at their primary residence, subject to the security and other conditions in [R.C. 3796.04](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3796.04). Ohio's adult-use/homegrown possession limit is cumulative: no more than 2.5 ounces of plant material and 15 grams of extract, counting homegrown and purchased adult-use cannabis together. Seeds, live plants and clones cultivated, grown or possessed in accordance with R.C. 3796.04 are excluded ([R.C. 3796.221(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3796.221)). The home-grow section authorizes storage at the primary residence and prohibits sale or profit. The transportation section separately permits transporting homegrown cannabis under its vehicle-storage conditions ([R.C. 3796.062(C)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3796.062)).

**Why growers ask about this.** A legal home grow can produce more than 2.5 ounces once harvested and dried, and the statute does not give a separate allowance for a home harvest.

**What isn't settled.** We have not found published guidance or a court decision establishing when harvested and drying plants stop counting as "plants being grown," or how the possession limit is enforced for home growers. Some online summaries describe a higher limit at home. We could not find that in the statute text. Last checked October 11, 2026.

The brief's sentence claiming that storing homegrown cannabis anywhere else is itself an offense under R.C. 3796.99 is omitted because that section does not substantiate it. No contrary permission to store anywhere is inferred.
