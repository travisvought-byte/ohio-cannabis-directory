Who can change what?

Ohio General Assembly

State representatives and senators can introduce and pass changes to Ohio’s cannabis statutes. Start here for consumption lounges, possession and transport protections, or changes to restrictions enacted through SB 56.

Official legislature and bill search · Current cannabis law

Division of Cannabis Control

DCC is part of the Ohio Department of Commerce. It administers the cannabis program and writes rules within the authority the legislature gives it. Use the rule-comment process for implementation details such as packaging, testing and permitted accessories.

ORC 3796.02 · ORC 3796.03

Joint Committee on Agency Rule Review

JCARR reviews agency rules and accepts public comments. It can recommend that the General Assembly invalidate a rule; the agency writes or amends the rule. Check the agenda before offering testimony.

Official comment guidance and meeting links

Municipal councils and township trustees

Local authorities can prohibit or limit the number of licensed cannabis businesses, subject to statutory exceptions. For a local restriction, request the ordinance or resolution and ask the clerk how to comment at a council or trustee meeting.

Local authority and exceptions: ORC 3796.29

Changes you can ask for

These are reform proposals. Each card links the current law so you can explain the change you want.

Licensed consumption lounges

Current law: Smoking or combustion of medical marijuana is prohibited. Adult-use and homegrown smoking, combustion and vaporization, and medical vaporization, are restricted to qualifying private residential or agricultural property, with additional location prohibitions.

Ask: Create a lawful licensing route for adult consumption lounges, with age checks, ventilation, worker protections and impaired-driving safeguards. This would require legislative changes to the location restrictions.

ORC 3796.06(C)

Revisit out-of-state product restrictions

Current law: Ohio’s marijuana definitions tie protected categories to Chapter 3796. Motor-vehicle transport is restricted to those categories.

Ask: Revisit Ohio penalties involving cannabis legally purchased in another state and give adults clearer protections. Ohio reform alone would not resolve federal restrictions on interstate transport.

ORC 3796.01(A)(2), (9), (17) · ORC 3796.062(A) · Federal possession law: 21 USC 844

Clear protections for ordinary accessories

Current law: Adult-use accessory protections depend on DCC rules. Marijuana paraphernalia has its own vehicle-transport conditions and penalty provisions.

Ask: Protect ordinary lawful-use accessories and replace confusing criminal exposure with clear, practical standards. This is not a claim that all pipes or accessories are banned.

ORC 3796.221(A)(3) · ORC 3796.062(D) · ORC 3796.99(J)

Home-grow rights

Ask: Protect adults’ ability to grow for personal use and seek clear, proportionate rules for possession, storage and lawful homegrown use. Explain the specific rule you want changed.

Current Chapter 3796

Medical patient access

Ask: Protect affordable, reliable patient access, including practical product options and accessibility. Tell lawmakers which cost or access barrier affects you.

Division of Cannabis Control

THC beverages and social venues

Ask: Create a lawful route for tested, clearly labeled THC beverages in licensed adult social venues, with age checks, serving limits, worker protections and impaired-driving safeguards. This is a proposal for legal change, not permission to sell or consume at a venue today. Federal restrictions also require separate attention.

SB 56 documents · Cannabis forms and use restrictions

Independent business opportunity and ownership transparency

Ask: Review licensing barriers, fees and market concentration; create practical opportunities for independent Ohio businesses; and publish understandable parent-company and controlling-ownership information. Ask officials how proposed changes affect small entrants and established operators.

Ohio-owned, Ohio-operated, or a multistate operator?

For people who want to support local ownership, the distinction matters. An Ohio address shows where a business operates. It does not establish who controls it or where its profits go.

How to read business descriptions
DescriptionWhat to check
Ohio-ownedCurrent evidence of Ohio ownership or control. A company’s own claim is labeled as a self-description, not an independent ownership audit.
Ohio-based or Ohio-operatedHeadquarters or operations in Ohio. Ownership may be local, outside Ohio, mixed or unknown.
Multistate operator (MSO)A cannabis operator with operations in more than one state. Identify its parent company and the particular brand or store.
IndependentCheck independence from a larger controlling operator. Private ownership, a local brand name or a single storefront does not establish independence.
UnconfirmedPublic evidence does not settle the question. Leave ownership unknown until it can be supported.

Two sourced examples

Klutch Cannabis: Ohio ownership self-description

Klutch’s official About page describes it as 100% Ohio-owned and operated and founded in Akron. This supports an attributed Ohio-ownership label; it is not a full beneficial-ownership audit.

Company About page · checked October 16, 2026

Green Thumb Industries: multistate operations

Green Thumb’s published retail terms identify participating company dispensaries in multiple states, including Ohio. This supports a multistate-operations label. Check the parent and current store information when evaluating an individual brand or location.

Company retail terms, section 26.2 · checked October 16, 2026

These examples explain the distinction; they are not a complete ownership directory or a ranking. Ohio ownership does not establish a company’s position on home grow, lounges or competition. Evaluate published policy positions separately.

Questions to ask before you shop or advocate

  • Who is the current parent company and who controls the license?
  • Is Ohio ownership documented, self-reported or unconfirmed?
  • Does the operator have businesses in other states?
  • What has the company actually said or supported about home grow, patient access, lounges and independent entrants?

Search the business directory and its sources · Review documented business relationships. Service-area labels describe reach, not ownership. A multistate service provider is not automatically a cannabis MSO.

Speak at a legislative hearing

Find the bill’s assigned committee and current hearing notice. Check the witness submission instructions and deadline before preparing testimony.

  1. Check the House committee listings or Senate committee guidance and schedule.
  2. State your name, whether you support or oppose the bill, one practical effect, and the specific change you request.
  3. Use the committee’s current witness form and submission instructions. Keep your testimony focused and bring supporting sources.

The SB 56 history shows its past committee route. A new reform bill can follow a different route; confirm its current assignment.

Comment on a proposed rule

  1. Search the Register of Ohio for Department of Commerce / Division of Cannabis Control filings.
  2. Open the proposed rule and public hearing notice. Confirm the rule number, deadline, hearing details and submission instructions in that notice.
  3. Explain which provision affects you, the practical problem, and the change you propose. Submit to the filing agency using the notice’s instructions.
  4. Check the JCARR guidance and agenda links if you also want to comment during its review.

Follow rule updates through RuleWatch Ohio. A proposed rule is not the same as an effective rule; check its filing status and effective date.
