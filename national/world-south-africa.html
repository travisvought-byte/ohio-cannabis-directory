Cannabis laws in South Africa

Planning a trip to South Africa, or live there? Adults: Yes, in private, since a 2018 court ruling · Buying: No legal shops. "Dispensaries" operate illegally · Carrying: No set amount yet. Draft rules propose 750 grams · At the border: No. Cannabis is a prohibited import.

Adults may use, have and grow cannabis in private because of a 2018 Constitutional Court ruling. Selling it is still a crime, and a 2024 law that would set the rules was signed but has not started. Medicines with THC are reached mostly through special permits.

The Cannabis for Private Purposes Act of 2024 is signed but not yet in force, so this page separates the rules that apply now from the ones waiting to start. Amounts are in South African rand.

What is different about South Africa

  • A court, not Parliament, opened the door. The 2018 Prince ruling rests on the constitutional right to privacy.
  • Private means more than home. The court said the right to privacy reaches beyond the boundaries of a home.
  • The law written to follow the ruling was signed in May 2024, but it starts only when the President proclaims it, and it had not by October 2026.
  • Until the rules start there is no legal amount. Police weigh the quantity and the circumstances, and a court decides whether it was for dealing.
  • There is no legal market. Selling stays a crime, and shops calling themselves dispensaries are unlicensed.
  • Once the 2024 Act starts, using cannabis around children or adults who object, or where the smoke drifts into a neighbour's window, is an offence.
  • Drug-driving law has no THC limit yet, and a 2019 study found drivers are rarely tested for drugs.

[1][2][3][4][5][6]

Travelling? Never carry cannabis across an international border, even between two places where it is legal. See where it can land you in prison.

The law in South Africa

Can adults use it without a prescription?

Limited Yes, in private, since a 2018 court ruling

On September 18, 2018 the Constitutional Court ruled in the Prince case that it is unconstitutional to make it a crime for an adult to use or possess cannabis in private for their own consumption, or to grow it in a private place for that purpose. The court said privacy reaches beyond the home. Using or possessing it in public, and any use by a child, stayed illegal. Parliament passed the Cannabis for Private Purposes Act in 2024 to put the ruling into law. It starts on a date the President sets by proclamation, and an October 2026 column in Business Day said it was still not in effect. [1][2][3]

Constitutional Court, Prince [2018] ZACC 30 (media summary); Cannabis for Private Purposes Act 7 of 2024 s. 8; Business Day, Oct 27 2026 (opinion) · checked November 19, 2026

Is there a medical program?

Limited Prescription, mostly through special permits

THC is a Schedule 6 medicine and CBD a Schedule 4 medicine, so both need a prescription. A pharmacist and pharmacologist writing in December 2025 said no THC-containing product had yet been registered in South Africa, so patients reach THC medicines through section 21 permits for unregistered products, and the regulator does not publish how many permits it issues. Low-dose CBD products, up to 20 mg a day, can be sold as complementary medicines. [4]

Spotlight, Dr Andy Gray, Dec 3 2025 (news column) · checked November 19, 2026

Where can adults buy it legally?

Not permitted No legal shops. "Dispensaries" operate illegally

The 2018 ruling did not legalise selling, and the 2024 Act keeps dealing a crime, with up to 10 years in prison. The medicines regulator has licensed no retail outlets for cannabis, and its cultivation permits do not allow sales to the public, so shops that call themselves licensed dispensaries are operating illegally, the Spotlight column says. [1][2][4]

Constitutional Court, Prince (media summary); Cannabis for Private Purposes Act 7 of 2024 s. 4; Spotlight, Dec 3 2025 (news column) · checked November 19, 2026

How much can an adult carry?

Limited No set amount yet. Draft rules propose 750 grams

For now there is no legal amount. The court left it to Parliament to set what counts as personal use, and police judge each case by the circumstances, including the quantity. If they suspect dealing they can arrest, and a court decides. Draft regulations published for comment in February 2026 propose up to 750 grams in a private place, and 750 grams a day in public if it is kept out of sight. Under the 2024 Act, once it starts, having more than the prescribed amount could bring up to five years in prison. [1][5][2][7]

Constitutional Court, Prince (media summary); Adams & Adams on the draft regulations, Feb 2026 (law firm); Cannabis for Private Purposes Act s. 4; EWN, Aug 21 2026 · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults share it?

Not permitted Not yet. The 2024 law would allow free sharing

The 2018 ruling covered using, possessing and growing for your own consumption. It did not cover selling or supplying anyone. Once in force, the 2024 Act lets one adult give cannabis to another in private with no payment or exchange of any kind. Giving it to a child is a crime unless a medical practitioner prescribed it. [1][2]

Constitutional Court, Prince (media summary); Cannabis for Private Purposes Act 7 of 2024 ss. 2 and 3 · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults grow it at home?

Limited Yes, in private. Draft rules propose 5 plants

Adults may grow cannabis in a private place for their own consumption in private. No plant limit is in force yet. The February 2026 draft regulations propose five plants per adult at any one time, regardless of size or strain. A lawyer quoted in August 2026 said the rules still need to make clear whether limits apply per person or per household. [1][5][7]

Constitutional Court, Prince (media summary); Adams & Adams on the draft regulations (law firm); EWN, Aug 21 2026 · checked November 19, 2026

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted Illegal while under the influence; no THC limit yet

The National Road Traffic Act bans driving under the influence of a drug having a narcotic effect. A 2019 study published through the University of Pretoria found that drivers are rarely tested for drugs and called for the law to spell out drug testing. The 2024 Act amends the road traffic law to add THC driving offences and limits, and, once it starts, makes using cannabis in a vehicle on a public road an offence with a fine of up to R2,000. [6][2]

Lienberg et al., Drugged driving in South Africa, 2019 (research); Cannabis for Private Purposes Act 7 of 2024 s. 4 and Schedule · checked November 19, 2026

Can an old cannabis record be cleared?

Limited Expungement is in the 2024 law, which has not started

The 2024 Act provides for expunging old convictions for using or possessing cannabis, and some dealing convictions that rested on a legal presumption. Some are to be wiped automatically; otherwise a person can apply in writing to the Director-General of Justice. The draft regulations say the Criminal Record Centre must confirm the expungement within 21 working days. None of this applies until the Act starts. [2][5]

Cannabis for Private Purposes Act 7 of 2024 s. 5; Adams & Adams on the draft regulations (law firm) · checked November 19, 2026

Can you take it across the border?

Not permitted No. Cannabis is a prohibited import

Customs guidance from the South African Revenue Service lists narcotic and psychotropic substances, including cannabis, among the goods that are illegal to bring into South Africa. The private-use rules stop at the border. [8]

SAinfo traveller's customs guide, from SARS guidance (reviewed 2015) · checked November 19, 2026

Good to know

The draft regulations go into detail on moving cannabis. They propose a limit of 750 grams per person a day in transit, including cannabis still on the plant, kept out of sight, preferably in the boot. Drivers would have to tell passengers it is on board before they get in. Public comment closed on March 5, 2026, and in August 2026 the rules were reported as set to be finalised soon. [5][7]
