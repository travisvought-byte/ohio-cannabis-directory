Budtender training guide: help every customer find the right product

Work the counter, or hiring people who do? Customers shouldn't have to know the menu to get good help. Here is a six-question intake that gets anyone from “I don’t know what I want” to the right product, with the pros and cons of every form.

Never make a customer name a category. Ask about the experience they want, and you name the category.

Six questions, in this order, get almost anyone to the right shelf. Bookmark this page, or print the quick cards for the counter.

  1. What do you want out of it?
  2. What are your requirements?
  3. What have you heard?
  4. Do you already have a favorite way?
  5. No preference? Walk through the options
  6. Point them to what others report

Also on this page: Quick cards · Customer scenarios · Good and bad, side by side · The rules of the counter · Sources

Step 1 of 6

What do you want out of it?

  • “What are you hoping it does for you?”
  • “Is this for a time of day, like evenings or weekends?”
  • “Anything you're trying to get help with?”

Listen for the outcome, not a product word: sleep, stress, aches, fun with friends, focus, appetite, or as little high as possible.

Why it matters: The goal decides strength and timing before you ever say flower or gummy. If someone mentions a health condition, take it as their goal in their words. You aren't diagnosing, and you can't promise relief.

Common goals and where they point

Wind down or sleep

Later in the day, longer lasting. Swallowed forms last longest; a low dose to start.

Take the edge off stress

Start low. Too much THC can bring on anxiety, so a lower THC or balanced THC:CBD product is a fair first step. [1]

Body aches or soreness

Ask what they've tried. Topicals, tinctures and edibles are common asks. Their doctor is the one to weigh in on pain treatment.

Social, fun, upbeat

Quick and short: a pre-roll to share, a low-dose gummy or a drink, at a pace they can manage.

Daytime, focus or creative

Low dose and short acting, so they stay functional. Inhaled forms wear off soonest.

Appetite

Ask about timing: before a meal points to something fast acting.

As little high as possible

CBD-dominant or balanced products, low THC per serving. Remind them CBD products can still contain some THC. [2]

Step 2 of 6

What are your requirements?

  • “Is smoke okay, or do you need smoke-free?”
  • “Does it need to be discreet?”
  • “How fast do you want to feel it, and how long should it last?”
  • “What's your budget today?”
  • “Do you get drug tested?”

Each answer rules forms in or out. Smoke-free rules out flower and pre-rolls. Fast rules out edibles. All night favors something swallowed.

Why it matters: Requirements are hard limits. Settle them first so you only show things that can work.

Smoke-free

Edibles, drinks, tinctures, capsules or topicals. Vapes are smoke-free but still inhaled.

Discreet

Gummies, capsules, tinctures and drinks look like everyday items and give off no smoke. Remind them to store them away from children.

Fast

Inhaled forms start in seconds to minutes. Edibles can take up to 2 hours. [2]

Long lasting

Swallowed forms last about 4 to 8 hours; inhaled forms about 2 to 4. [2]

Budget

Ask for a number. Then compare by milligrams of THC per dollar or by the gram, not by the sticker price.

Drug tested

Be honest: any THC product can lead to a positive test, CBD products included. The US Department of Transportation says CBD use is no excuse for a positive. [3]

Step 3 of 6

What have you heard?

  • “Have you tried anything before? How did it go?”
  • “Anything you've heard that you want to avoid, or want to try?”

Listen for the common beliefs below. Clear them up gently, without making anyone feel dumb.

Why it matters: A bad first experience usually starts with a belief, like taking a second gummy because the first one didn't hit yet.

What they heard

“Indica means couch, sativa means energy.”

What’s true A study of nearly 90,000 flower samples from six states found the labels don't consistently match the chemistry; samples labeled indica, sativa and hybrid were highly intermingled. [4]

Try saying “Those labels are a rough start. The lab report on this batch tells us more.”

What they heard

“Higher THC is always better.”

What’s true Health Canada advises new users to start under 10% THC for inhaled products and to take the most care above 20%, which includes shatter, wax and distillates. [1]

Try saying “More THC means more chance of a rough time. Let's match the strength to your experience.”

What they heard

“The edible didn't hit, so take another.”

What’s true Swallowed cannabis can take 30 minutes to 2 hours to start working and peaks after 2 to 4 hours. That delay is why people take too much. [2]

Try saying “Give it at least two hours before you take more.”

What they heard

“It's the same stuff I smoked years ago.”

What’s true In federal testing of seized cannabis, average THC rose from about 4% in 1995 to about 16% in 2022. [5]

Try saying “Today's flower is a lot stronger than what you remember. Start lower than you think.”

What they heard

“CBD can't show up on a drug test.”

What’s true The US Department of Transportation warns that CBD labels can be misleading, products may contain more THC than stated, and CBD use is no excuse for a confirmed positive. [3]

Try saying “If you're tested, there's no product I can promise won't show.”

What they heard

“A cart is a cart, wherever you get it.”

What’s true In the 2019 vaping lung injury outbreak, 82% of patients reported using THC vapes, vitamin E acetate was strongly linked, and the CDC warned against THC vapes from informal sources such as friends or dealers. [6]

Try saying “Licensed carts are tested and tracked. Street carts aren't.”

What they heard

“The strain name guarantees the effect.”

What’s true Evidence that terpenes and other compounds steer the effect in people, the so-called entourage effect, is still early, with simple and contradictory studies. [7]

Try saying “People report patterns, but every body is different. Let's start small and you tell me how it goes.”

Step 4 of 6

Do you already have a favorite way?

  • “Do you already know how you like to take it?”
  • “Any flavor, brand or strain you liked before?”

If they have a preference, go straight to it and narrow by strength, size, flavor and price.

Why it matters: Respect what they know. Don't send a happy flower customer through the whole speech.

Step 5 of 6

No preference? Walk through the options

  • “Want me to walk you through the main options? It takes a minute.”

Use the product cards below: how it works, how fast, how long, pros and cons. Offer two or three that fit their requirements, not all eight.

Why it matters: This is the part customers are really asking for when they say they don't know what they want.

Flower

Dried buds, smoked in a pipe, bong or joint or heated in a dry herb vaporizer.

Feel it
Seconds to minutes
Lasts
About 2 to 4 hours [2]

Pros

  • Fast, so it's easy to stop when you've had enough
  • Widest choice of strains and prices
  • You can smell and see what you're buying

Cons

  • Smoke and smell
  • Needs gear: papers, a pipe or a vaporizer
  • Dose is less exact

Good fit: People who want control and speed, and don't mind smoke.

Pre-rolls

Flower already rolled into a joint. Infused pre-rolls add concentrate and are much stronger.

Feel it
Seconds to minutes
Lasts
About 2 to 4 hours [2]

Pros

  • Ready to go, no gear but a lighter
  • Easy to share
  • Small sizes are a cheap way to try a strain

Cons

  • Smoke and smell
  • You can't see the flower inside
  • Infused ones surprise new users

Good fit: Social use, trying a strain, people who don't roll.

Vapes and carts

A cartridge of cannabis oil on a battery, or an all-in-one disposable. Heated, not burned.

Feel it
Seconds to minutes
Lasts
About 2 to 4 hours [2][6]

Pros

  • No smoke, less smell
  • Portable and quick
  • One puff at a time makes pacing easy

Cons

  • Usually far stronger than flower
  • Needs a compatible battery
  • Buy licensed only: the 2019 lung injury outbreak was tied to THC vapes, many from informal sources

Good fit: Discreet, quick use by people who are comfortable with stronger products.

Concentrates

Resin pulled from the plant into wax, shatter, badder, rosin or live resin, dabbed with a rig or a concentrate vaporizer.

Feel it
Seconds to minutes
Lasts
About 2 to 4 hours [1]

Pros

  • A little goes a long way
  • Strong flavor in live resin and rosin
  • Good value per milligram for experienced users

Cons

  • Very strong: Health Canada puts products over 20% THC in its most-caution band
  • Needs special gear
  • Not a first-timer product

Good fit: Experienced, high-tolerance customers.

Edibles and gummies

Food with cannabis in it, digested before it works. The liver changes THC into a form called 11-hydroxy-THC.

Feel it
30 minutes to 2 hours
Lasts
About 4 to 8 hours, sometimes longer [2][1]

Pros

  • No smoke, no smell
  • Exact milligrams per piece
  • Long lasting, good for nights

Cons

  • Slow start, so people take too much
  • Hard to stop once it's working
  • Look like candy: store away from kids

Good fit: Smoke-free, discreet, long-lasting use. Start at 2.5 mg THC if new.

Drinks

Cannabis in a beverage. It's swallowed, so it behaves like an edible.

Feel it
30 minutes to 2 hours
Lasts
About 4 to 8 hours [2][1]

Pros

  • Social, like having a drink
  • Low doses are common
  • No smoke

Cons

  • Still slow to start
  • Don't mix with alcohol: it adds to impairment

Good fit: People who want a social, low-dose option.

Tinctures, oils and capsules

Liquid drops or capsules. Held under the tongue, drops start sooner; swallowed, they act like an edible.

Feel it
15 to 40 minutes under the tongue; 30 minutes to 2 hours swallowed
Lasts
Up to about 12 hours under the tongue [2]

Pros

  • Easy to adjust a drop at a time
  • No smoke, discreet
  • Many balanced THC:CBD options

Cons

  • Taste of some oils
  • Still slower than inhaling
  • Read the label: drops per serving vary

Good fit: Careful dosing, CBD-curious and medical customers.

Topicals

Creams, balms and lotions applied to the skin.

Feel it
Not well studied
Lasts
Not well studied [2]

Pros

  • Applied right where they want it
  • No smoke
  • Easy to try

Cons

  • Health Canada says there isn't yet enough information on timing, duration or risks
  • Don't promise a body effect like an edible

Good fit: People who want something on the skin, not a high.

Step 6 of 6

Point them to what others report

  • “Here's what people often pick for that. The lab report for this batch tells us more than the name does.”

Offer names customers commonly ask for, then check the batch label for THC, CBD and terpenes.

Why it matters: A strain name and an indica or sativa label are weak predictors. Frame names as what other customers report, never as a promise.

These are what other customers and breeders report, not medical claims or guarantees. The same name can mean different plants from different growers, so the batch label is the final word.

Wind down or sleep

  • Northern Lights: Weedmaps reviewers describe physical relaxation, with sleepiness a frequent report. [8]

Whatever the strain, a later time of day and a swallowed form matter more for an all-night effect.

Daytime energy, focus or creativity

  • Durban Poison: Weedmaps reviewers describe a fast, bright high with energy, focus and creativity. [9]
  • Jack Herer: Weedmaps reviewers most often report uplifted, happy and relaxed. Sensi Seeds, its breeder, says one version of the plant is the most energetic and another the most physical. [10][11]

Keep the dose low for daytime so they stay functional.

Social and upbeat

  • Blue Dream: Humboldt Seed Organization, a breeder of one Blue Dream line, describes a clear, long-lasting cerebral effect. [12]

Shared pre-rolls and low-dose drinks are easy ways to pace a group.

Little or no high (CBD-rich)

  • ACDC: About 20 parts CBD to 1 part THC; Weedmaps reviewers most often report relaxed, happy and uplifted. [13][14]
  • Harlequin: Project CBD lists it among early CBD-rich strains at about 3 parts CBD to 2 parts THC. [13]
  • Cannatonic: Its breeder, Resin Seeds, says plants range from 30:1 THC:CBD to 1:30, so the batch label decides. [15]

CBD-rich flower is rare: 96.5% of the tested flower in that six-state study was THC-dominant. Balanced tinctures and gummies are easier to find. [4]

Look up a strain’s reported family tree in the strain library, and learn how to read a lab report.

Start low, go slow

  • Edibles: New users: 2.5 mg THC or less. 2.5 to 10 mg, use caution. 10 mg or more, the most caution. [1]
  • Inhaled: Start with 1 or 2 puffs and wait before taking more. [2]
  • Inhaled strength: New users: under 10% THC, with equal or more CBD. 10% to 20%, use caution. Over 20%, the most caution. [1]
  • Drops: Start with 1 drop and go up one drop at a time. [2]

Customer scenarios

The same six questions, applied to the people who walk in every day.

First-timer

They say
"I've never done this. What should I get?"
You ask
What do you want it to do? Smoke okay? Do you want to feel it fast or slow?
Steer to
A low-dose gummy (2.5 mg) or one or two puffs of low-THC flower. Explain the edible wait out loud.

Coming back after years away

They say
"I used to smoke in college. Give me the strongest."
You ask
When did you last use it? What did you like then?
Steer to
Tell them average THC roughly quadrupled since the mid-1990s. Steer to moderate flower and go up from there.

Medical patient

They say
"My doctor said this could help with my back."
You ask
What has your doctor suggested? What have you tried? Do you take other medicines?
Steer to
Match their requirements and comfort level. Send medicine-interaction questions to their doctor or pharmacist; CBD and THC can both interact.

Buying for someone else

They say
"It's for my dad. He's never tried it."
You ask
What does he want it to do? Does he smoke? Any health issues or medicines he's mentioned?
Steer to
Smoke-free and low dose, with the timing written down. Remind them that only adults 21 and over may buy, and to store it away from kids.

Had a bad experience

They say
"Last time I got way too high and freaked out."
You ask
What did you take, how much, and how fast did it hit?
Steer to
Usually too much THC or a second edible too soon. Offer lower THC, a balanced THC:CBD product, and the start-low rules.

On a budget

They say
"What's cheapest?"
You ask
What's your number, and how do you like to use it?
Steer to
Compare by THC milligrams per dollar or price per gram. Larger flower sizes, value brands and daily deals.

Needs it discreet

They say
"I can't have it smell. I live with my parents."
You ask
Smoke-free okay? How long do you need it to last?
Steer to
Gummies, capsules or tinctures. No smoke, no smell, exact doses.

Gets drug tested

They say
"Will CBD show up on a test?"
You ask
Do you get tested for work?
Steer to
Be straight: CBD products can contain THC and can cause a positive. Don't promise any product is test-safe.

The rules of the counter

Check ID on every sale

Ohio requires a dispensary employee to review a valid government photo ID with a date of birth before any adult-use sale, and confirm the buyer is 21 or older. [16]

Know the daily limit

Ohio's adult-use daily limit is 2.5 ounces of plant material and no more than 15,000 mg of total THC. Other states differ. [16] Buying rules in every state.

Don't drive high

Say it plainly with every edible, and don't mix with alcohol, which adds to impairment. [1]

Lock it up at home

Edibles can be mistaken for normal food. Tell customers with kids or pets to store products locked away. [1]

Know who to call

If someone has worrying symptoms after taking cannabis, US Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. For an emergency, call 911. [17]

Leave medical calls to clinicians

Don't diagnose or promise relief. CBD can have side effects and interact with medicines, so send those questions to a doctor or pharmacist. [18]

Use your store's materials

Ohio dispensaries must keep educational materials on substance abuse and access to addiction services. Know where yours are. [19]
