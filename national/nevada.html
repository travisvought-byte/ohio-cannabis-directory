The law in Nevada

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Adults 21 and older can legally possess up to 2.5 ounces of cannabis and up to 1/4 ounce of concentrate.

Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, Laws and regulations · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

The Cannabis Compliance Board oversees medical cannabis, including patient ID cards.

Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed State-licensed retail stores, with ID

Adults may buy cannabis only from state-licensed retail stores and must show ID proving they are 21 or older.

Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, Laws and regulations · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 2.5 ounces; 1/4 ounce concentrate

Adults 21 and older can legally possess up to 2.5 ounces of cannabis and up to 1/4 ounce of concentrated cannabis.

Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, Laws and regulations · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 2.5 oz or 1/4 oz concentrate, free

An adult may deliver up to 2.5 ounces of usable cannabis or 1/4 ounce of concentrated cannabis to another adult 21 or older without remuneration, if the delivery is not advertised or promoted.

NRS 678D.200(3)(f) · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Limited Only if no store within 25 miles

Adults 21 and older may grow up to six plants each and 12 per household, but only if no state-licensed retail cannabis store is within 25 miles of the home.

Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, Laws and regulations · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted No

Cannabis cannot be used in any public place, or in a moving vehicle even as a passenger.

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Job applicants and medical patients

It is unlawful for a Nevada employer to refuse to hire a prospective employee because a screening test shows marijuana. That does not cover applicants to be firefighters or emergency medical technicians, jobs that require driving and for which law requires screening tests, or jobs the employer determines could adversely affect the safety of others. It gives way to an employment contract or collective bargaining agreement and to federal law, and does not cover positions funded by a federal grant. The Marijuana Policy Project, an advocacy group, lists no protection for current employees’ adult use, and lists a duty to try to reasonably accommodate medical cardholders’ use away from work (NRS 678C.850); those two points are not yet checked against the state’s own law.

NRS 613.132; Marijuana Policy Project’s table and chart for the other points · checked November 15, 2026 [4][5][6] Official source and national compilation

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Decriminalized offenses, by written request

Nevada law lets a person convicted of an offense that was later decriminalized, by an act of the Legislature or a voter initiative, send a written request to any court where they were convicted to seal the records of that conviction held by the court and by criminal justice agencies. No court or agency may charge a fee. The court notifies the prosecutor, who has 10 judicial days to object in writing; with no objection the court must grant the request, and after an objection it holds a hearing and grants the request unless the prosecutor shows good cause by clear and convincing evidence. The section covers decriminalized offenses in general and does not name marijuana; it does not apply to traffic offenses.

NRS 179.271 (Nevada Revised Statutes, revised April 15, 2026) · checked November 15, 2026 [7] Official source
