Is weed legal in Oregon?

Short answer: Yes. Weed is legal in Oregon for adults 21 and older, and for medical patients.

Checked November 15, 2026 against Oregon Health Authority, Cannabis laws in Oregon [1]

If you are 21 or older in Oregon, here is the short version. Buy: OLCC-licensed retailers · Carry: 2 ounces in public, 8 at home · Grow at home: 4 plants. How patients get a medical card in Oregon

From Oregon Health Authority, Oregon Medical Marijuana Program. Adults 21 and older can also buy without a card; see the answers below.

Questions: the program’s customer service line is open 12:00 to 4:00 PM, Monday to Friday. [2]

Steps

  1. Have your provider sign the Attending Provider’s Statement.
  2. Create an account and apply online at ommpsystem.oregon.gov, or apply by mail.
  3. Submit the application, the provider statement, a copy of your photo ID and the fee. Online applicants can print a 30-day receipt once the application is complete.

Steps as the state describes them [2]

Who can get one
Oregon residents with an Attending Provider’s Statement signed within 90 days of applying. [2]
Cost
$200 basic application fee when you grow for yourself or list no grower. [3]
Help with the cost
Reduced fees with current proof: $60 with SNAP, $50 with the Oregon Health Plan, $20 with SSI, $20 for veterans. No fee for veterans with a service-connected total disability rating of at least 50 percent and a discharge under other than dishonorable conditions. [3]
Caregivers
You can designate a caregiver on your application; include a copy of the caregiver’s photo ID. The caregiver’s card is mailed to you. [2]

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Not protected Depends on the employer’s policies

The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission says the state’s cannabis law does not change existing employment law. Employers who test for drugs may keep doing so, and whether a person can be fired for using marijuana depends on the employer’s policies.

Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission, Frequently asked questions · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Qualifying convictions, no fee or waiting period

Oregon’s courts say ORS 475C.397 requires a court to set aside a qualifying marijuana conviction, with no waiting period and no filing fee, unless the prosecutor objects within 30 days that the conviction does not qualify. On the statewide form a person declares that the conduct took place before July 1, 2015 and that the conviction was for conduct described in ORS 475C.305 or for possessing less than one ounce of dried marijuana. The same form asks the court under ORS 137.222 to lower a conviction for an offense that has since been reduced. A set-aside seals the record and the person is treated as not previously convicted. Separately, on November 21, 2022 the Governor pardoned state-court convictions for possessing one ounce or less in pre-2015 cases where the person was 21 or older, it was the only charge and there was no victim; those records were sealed without an application.

ORS 475C.397 and ORS 137.222, per the Oregon Judicial Department’s marijuana set-aside motion form (July 2023) · checked November 15, 2026 [7][8] Official source
