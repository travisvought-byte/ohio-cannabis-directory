The law in Colorado

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

It is illegal for anyone under 21 to buy, have or use retail cannabis. Adults 21 and older can have up to 2 ounces.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

Colorado’s state site says medical cannabis has its own use and possession rules. DISA’s national map also lists medical use as legal.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use; DISA national map · checked November 15, 2026 [1][2] Official source and national compilation

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed stores, 1 ounce at a time

Buy retail cannabis only from licensed retail stores, up to 1 ounce at a time, with valid ID showing you are 21 or older.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 2 ounces

Adults 21 and older can have up to 2 ounces. Having more can bring charges and fines.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 2 ounces, no sales

Adults may give up to 2 ounces to another adult 21 or older but may not sell cannabis, including homegrown cannabis.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 6 plants per resident 21+, 3 flowering

Each Colorado resident 21 or older may grow up to six plants, with up to three flowering at a time, in an enclosed, locked area that cannot be viewed openly. Local laws can be stricter; Denver caps a home at 12 plants.

State of Colorado, Home grow laws · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted Not in public

Use is not allowed in public places such as sidewalks, parks and restaurants, or on federal land, including national parks and forests.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted DUI applies; 5 ng/mL THC inference

A driver affected to the slightest degree can be arrested for DUI or DWAI. A blood test of 5 nanograms or more of active THC per milliliter lets a court assume the driver was under the influence. Neither drivers nor passengers may open cannabis packaging and use the product in a vehicle, even when it is not moving.

State of Colorado, Driving and traveling · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Not protected Employers may act on a THC drug test

Colorado’s cannabis site says that even though cannabis is legal, employers can still test for THC and make employment decisions based on the results. It advises people to learn their workplace’s policies before using cannabis.

State of Colorado, Laws about cannabis use, “Drug testing at work” · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Many drug offenses; motion for now-legal conduct

Colorado’s courts publish a no-fee motion to seal a conviction for conduct that state law no longer prohibits (forms JDF 2370, 2371 and 2374, under C.R.S. § 24-72-711): it is filed in the criminal case once the sentence and any supervision are complete, with a copy to the prosecutor, needs no criminal history report, and agencies generally seal within 30 days of the order. The forms do not name marijuana. The Marijuana Policy Project, an advocacy group, lists a separate Clean Slate law that seals many drug misdemeanors and felonies after waiting periods, automatically from 2024, and 2020 pardons for possession of up to two ounces; those points are not yet checked against the state’s own law.

C.R.S. § 24-72-711, per the Colorado Judicial Branch’s forms JDF 2370 and JDF 2374 (July 1, 2025); Marijuana Policy Project’s table of legalization states’ expungement laws · checked November 15, 2026 [5][6][7] Official source and national compilation
