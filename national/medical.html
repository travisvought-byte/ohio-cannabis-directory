Getting a medical cannabis card, state by state

In 15 states, medical cannabis is legal but adult use is not, so a state card is the only legal way to buy. Each state below links to its patient guide: who qualifies, the steps, the cost and whether visiting patients can buy. Every fact comes from the state regulator.

Paying for it

Medicaid, Medicare and insurance generally do not pay for medical cannabis, but some states cut the card fee. See who pays and where the card costs less.

States where adults can buy

Every adult-use state also has a medical program, often with lower taxes or higher limits for patients. Patient guides so far:

AlaskaCost: $25 for an initial application; $20 for a timely renewal, before the current card expires.ArizonaCost: $150 for a qualifying patient application. A minor patient application, which includes the caregiver, is $350. Renewals cost the same as new applications.CaliforniaCost: Set by each county, up to $100 per application or renewal.ColoradoCost: $52 per application, non-refundable.ConnecticutCost: No fee for patients or caregivers to register.Visitors: Since October 1, 2026, patients and caregivers registered for medical cannabis in another state or jurisdiction may purchase medical cannabis in Connecticut.DelawareCost: $50 for one year, $75 for two years or $100 for three years. Non-refundable.District of ColumbiaCost: Free for District residents. Non-residents pay $10 to $100, depending on the period they choose.Visitors: Non-residents 21 or older can register by self-certifying. Patients from jurisdictions with reciprocity can also use their home registration; ABCA lists the eligible jurisdictions.IllinoisVeterans who get their care at a VA facility do not need a physician’s written certification. They submit their VA medical records for the last 12 months instead.MaineVisitors: Visiting patients show their home state’s registry card or other state-issued patient document, if their home state allows it. They may buy up to 2.5 ounces every 15 days. The Office lists the approved states.MarylandCost: $25 for the patient ID card, ordered after approval.MassachusettsCost: No fee for a registration card. A replacement card is $10, and that fee can be waived for verified financial hardship. Providers set their own fees for certification visits.MichiganCost: $40 application fee.MinnesotaCost: None. The annual enrollment fee was eliminated effective July 1, 2023.MissouriVisitors: A person with an equivalent card or authorization from another state is not subject to arrest under Missouri law for possessing less than the limits set for Missouri patients.MontanaCost: $20 for a new or renewal registration; $10 for a replacement card. Fees are non-refundable.NevadaCost: $50 for a one-year registration or $100 for two years. The registry says the fee cannot be waived or paid in installments, and insurance does not cover it.New JerseyCost: Digital ID cards are free. A physical card costs $10.New MexicoCost: None. The Department of Health charges no fee to enroll, take part or use the patient portal.New YorkVisitors: Out-of-state patients can buy with proof of a certification, registration or equivalent from their state and a government-issued photo ID. Confirm requirements with the dispensary.OhioWhere patients buy: Licensed dispensaries. The Division publishes a map of them.OregonCost: $200 basic application fee when you grow for yourself or list no grower.Rhode IslandCost: No fee for a patient, caregiver or authorized purchaser application.VermontCost: $50 with each initial or renewal application, non-refundable.VirginiaWhere patients buy: Licensed medical cannabis dispensaries.WashingtonCost: At least $1 for the recognition card.
