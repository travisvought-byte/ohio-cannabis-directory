Visiting Boston? The cannabis rules for visitors

In Boston for a game, a college visit or a weekend? Adults 21 and over can buy at licensed stores with a government-issued ID and carry up to 2 ounces since April 2026, but not use it in public, not with an open container in the car and not outside Massachusetts. Here is what the rules say, each from the agency that sets them.

Can tourists buy weed in Boston?

Yes, if you are 21 or older with a valid government-issued ID, at a store licensed by the Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission. You have to use what you buy in Massachusetts.

  • You must be 21 or older to buy cannabis in Massachusetts, and you need a government-issued ID. [1]
  • Out-of-state visitors can buy cannabis if they are 21 or older with a valid government-issued ID, but they must enjoy their purchase in Massachusetts. [1]
  • All marijuana establishments and medical treatment centers, delivery businesses included, must be licensed by the Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission. [2]

How do I find a licensed cannabis store in Boston?

Use the Cannabis Control Commission’s Where to Buy search, which covers licensed marijuana establishments, medical treatment centers and delivery businesses.

  • The Commission’s Where to Buy page searches licensed medical treatment centers and marijuana establishments, delivery businesses included. [2] Find a licensed store on the Cannabis Control Commission’s list

How much weed can I have in Massachusetts?

Up to 2 ounces on you since April 19, 2026, and up to 10 ounces at home. A store can sell you up to 2 ounces a day, or the equivalent in concentrates or edibles.

  • Adults 21 and older can carry up to two ounces of cannabis and possess up to 10 ounces at home. Anything over two ounces at home must be locked up. [1]
  • The two-ounce limit comes from An Act Modernizing the Commonwealth’s Cannabis Laws, which the Governor signed on April 19, 2026 and which took effect immediately. Before that, the limit was one ounce. [3][4]
  • Under the Commission’s Administrative Order No. 6, a store may sell a customer no more than two ounces a day, which equals 10 grams of active THC in concentrates or 1,000 milligrams of active THC in edibles. [5]

Where can you smoke weed in Boston?

In a private home where the owner allows it. Not in any public place, including parks, sidewalks, bars and restaurants, not in a car and not on federal land or federal waters.

  • It is illegal to use cannabis in any form in public places or on federal land. [1]
  • The Boston Public Health Commission says the law prohibits smoking or consuming cannabis of any kind in any public place, including public parks, sidewalks, bars and restaurants. [6]
  • Smoking cannabis is prohibited anywhere smoking tobacco is prohibited. [7]
  • Employers, landlords, cities and towns may have their own cannabis policies. [1]

Can I smoke weed in my Boston hotel room?

Only if the hotel allows it. The Cannabis Control Commission tells visitors to ask a hotel or other rental about its cannabis policy before using any.

  • If you’re staying at a hotel or other rental accommodation, the Commission says to ask about its marijuana use policies before consuming. [1]
  • Landlords may have their own cannabis policies. [1]

Can I use cannabis at a lounge in Boston?

Only at a business with a state social consumption license, in a city or town that has opted in. Massachusetts’ rules took effect January 2, 2026, before any licenses were available.

  • Massachusetts’ social consumption regulations took effect on January 2, 2026. At that point licenses were not yet available, and businesses may not sell cannabis for on-site consumption until they receive a state license. [8]
  • A city or town must opt in, by referendum, ordinance or bylaw, before social consumption businesses can operate there. [8]
  • The rules create three license types: Supplemental, Hospitality and Event Organizer. [8]

Can I have weed in a car in Massachusetts, or drive after using it?

Keep it in a closed container in the trunk or a locked glove compartment. An open container in the passenger area is illegal, and so is driving high.

  • It is illegal to have an open container of cannabis in a car’s passenger area, with a civil penalty of up to $500. Store it in a closed container in the trunk or a locked glove compartment. [1]
  • It is illegal to drive under the influence of cannabis. [1][9]

Can I take weed on a boat or onto federal land from Boston?

No. The Cannabis Control Commission says cannabis can’t be possessed on, used in or carried through federal waterways, such as oceans and rivers, and using it on federal land is illegal.

  • Cannabis cannot be possessed, consumed in or transported through federal waterways. Federally regulated areas, including waterways such as oceans or rivers, and airspace, remain under federal restrictions. [7]
  • It is illegal to use cannabis in any form on federal land. [1]

Can I fly home with weed from Boston?

No. Massachusetts says it is illegal to take cannabis out of the state by plane, train, boat or any other way, or to mail it.

  • It is illegal to drive across state lines with cannabis, or to take it outside Massachusetts by plane, train, boat or any other mode of transportation. You can’t mail it either. [1]
  • TSA says marijuana and some cannabis products, including some CBD oil, remain illegal under federal law unless they have no more than 0.3% THC on a dry-weight basis or are FDA-approved. Does Massachusetts accept medical cannabis cards from other states?

No. Only registered patients who live in Massachusetts, or their caregivers, can buy medical cannabis there. Visitors 21 and older can still buy at adult-use stores.

  • Out-of-state residents cannot buy medical marijuana in Massachusetts. Only registered qualifying patients who are Massachusetts residents, or their caregivers, can. [1]

How much of an edible should I take?

Start low and go slow. The Cannabis Control Commission says edibles can take one to four hours to take effect.

  • Edibles can take one to four hours to take effect. Check the label, start with a low dose and wait before taking more. [9]
  • For a possible poisoning, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222. [9]
