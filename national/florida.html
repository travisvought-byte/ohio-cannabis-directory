Is weed legal in Florida?

In Florida, only registered patients can buy and use cannabis, so for most people the first step is a medical card. Who qualifies, the steps, what it costs and where patients buy, as the state regulator (Florida Department of Health, Office of Medical Marijuana Use) gives them, are just below, followed by the list of dispensing locations the Office of Medical Marijuana Use lists. Old cannabis record holding you back? In Florida: Court sealing or expungement, once in a lifetime.

How patients get a medical card in Florida

From Florida Department of Health, Office of Medical Marijuana Use. Adults without a card cannot buy here.

Steps

  1. See a qualified physician. The physician diagnoses a qualifying condition and enters you in the state’s Medical Marijuana Use Registry.
  2. Apply for your registry ID card online or by mail.
  3. Once approved, fill your physician’s order at a licensed medical marijuana treatment center, in person or by delivery.

Steps as the state describes them [1]

Who can get one
Permanent or seasonal Florida residents diagnosed with a qualifying medical condition by a qualified physician. [2]
Cost
$75 per application (online payments add $2.75). [1]
How long it lasts
Renew every year. Renewals open 45 days before the card expires. [3]
Caregivers
Allowed. Minor patients must name a caregiver, and every caregiver needs their own registry card. [1]
Visiting patients
Only permanent or seasonal Florida residents can get a Florida card. [2]
Where patients buy
Only at state-approved medical marijuana treatment centers, which may also deliver. [1]

The law in Florida

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Medical patients only No

Florida has no adult-use law. Possessing 20 grams of cannabis or less is a first-degree misdemeanor; larger amounts are felonies.

Fla. Stat. 893.13(6) · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

Medical marijuana and low-THC cannabis are available to qualified patients. A qualified physician orders them, and licensed medical marijuana treatment centers are the only businesses allowed to dispense them.

Florida Office of Medical Marijuana Use · checked November 15, 2026 [6] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Medical patients only Patients and caregivers, at licensed treatment centers

Only licensed medical marijuana treatment centers can sell medical marijuana, and only to a qualified patient or the patient’s caregiver. It may not be purchased anywhere else.

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Not protected No workplace accommodation; drug-free policies allowed

Florida’s medical marijuana law says it does not limit an employer’s drug-free workplace program or policy, does not require an employer to accommodate medical use in any workplace or an employee working under the influence, and creates no claim for wrongful discharge or discrimination. Case law on off-duty use was not checked.

Fla. Stat. § 381.986(15) · checked November 23, 2026 [8] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Court sealing or expungement, once in a lifetime

Florida seals or expunges a criminal history record by court order. A person first applies to the Florida Department of Law Enforcement for a certificate of eligibility (a notarized application, fingerprints, certified dispositions and a $75 processing fee), then petitions the court; the Department says a record gets no relief until it receives a certified court order, and a person is entitled to one court-ordered sealing or expungement in a lifetime under ss. 943.0585 and 943.059. Separately, s. 943.0595 seals records automatically, with no limit on how many times, when no charge was filed, all counts were dismissed or dropped, or the person was acquitted of all counts, except for forcible felonies and some other offenses; the clerk sends the disposition to the Department, which seals its record. The law covers criminal records in general; none of the sources mentions marijuana.

ss. 943.0585, 943.059 and 943.0595, F.S.; Florida Department of Law Enforcement, Seal and Expunge Process · checked November 16, 2026 [9][10][11] Official source
