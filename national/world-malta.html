Cannabis laws in Malta

Planning a trip to Malta, or live there? Adults: Yes, 18 and older, since December 2021 · Buying: No shops. Non-profit associations, residents only · Carrying: 7 grams on you, 50 grams at home · At the border: No. Importing is a crime, even for your own use. Where to join (licensed associations) is just below.

The first EU country to legalize personal use, in December 2021. There are no shops and no legal route for visitors: adults can carry 7 grams, grow four plants at home, or join a licensed non-profit association if they live in Malta.

What is different about Malta

  • First in Europe. The law took effect on December 18, 2021.
  • No shops, and nothing for visitors. The legal sources are your own four plants or a non-profit association, and associations are for people who live in Malta.
  • Small by design. Associations are capped at 500 members and may not advertise or put up signs, and only 25 held an operating permit when we checked.
  • Smell counts. Since 2025, cannabis odour that causes a nuisance to others carries the same €235 penalty as using it in public, and ARUC inspectors handle complaints about private homes.
  • Weaker cannabis for the youngest members. ARUC's head said THC must not exceed 18 percent for members aged 18 to 21.
  • Driving has a hard number: 2 micrograms of THC per litre of blood for private drivers, with random roadside tests since March 2026.
  • An old conviction for something now decriminalised comes off the conduct certificate on a written request.
  • The border has a surrender rule. A visitor who hands the drug to police or customs at the first opportunity after landing, and declares it was for personal use, is exempt from criminal liability if the amount fits personal use. Otherwise, bringing it in is importation, a crime.

[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]

Travelling? Never carry cannabis across an international border, even between two places where it is legal. See where it can land you in prison.

Where to join: licensed associations

Malta has no shops. The regulator publishes the associations that hold an operating permit, and only these may enrol members. An ID card or residence card issued by the Maltese Government is needed to join, and the regulator says tourists may not become members. [10]

The law in Malta

Can adults use it without a prescription?

Allowed Yes, 18 and older, since December 2021

Act LXVI of 2021 took effect on December 18, 2021. Since then a person over 18 may have up to 7 grams for personal use without committing an offence. The same law set up ARUC, the Authority for the Responsible Use of Cannabis, to regulate the system. [11][3][2][12]

Act LXVI of 2021; Cap. 537 art. 4A; Government of Malta · checked November 19, 2026

Is there a medical program?

Allowed Yes, by prescription since 2018

Any licensed doctor may prescribe medicinal cannabis when there is no viable alternative. Applications are made patient by patient, as directed by the Superintendent of Public Health, and a pharmacist dispenses it at a licensed pharmacy. Preparations may not be meant for smoking. The Malta Medicines Authority publishes the list of approved products. [3][13][14]

Cap. 537 art. 10; Superintendence of Public Health; Malta Medicines Authority · checked November 19, 2026

Where can adults buy it legally?

Limited No shops. Non-profit associations, residents only

The only legal supply is a non-profit Cannabis Harm Reduction Association licensed by ARUC; 25 held an operating permit when we checked. Members must be at least 18 and show an identity or residence card issued by the Maltese government, and ARUC says tourists may not become members. A member can get at most 7 grams a day and 50 grams a month, plus up to 20 seeds a month. Each association is capped at 500 members, may not advertise, and must be at least 250 metres from schools, sports facilities and youth centres. [3][4][15]

Cap. 537 art. 7A; ARUC; BusinessNow.mt (news) · checked November 19, 2026

How much can an adult carry?

Allowed 7 grams on you, 50 grams at home

Adults may carry up to 7 grams. More than 7 and up to 28 grams for personal use goes before a Commissioner for Justice, with a penalty of €50 to €100. At home, up to 50 grams of dried cannabis is allowed. Using it in a public place costs €235, and using it in front of anyone under 18 costs €300 to €500. [3]

Cap. 537 arts. 4A and 7 · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults share it?

Not permitted No. Supplying counts as dealing

The 2021 reform covers having, growing and getting cannabis from an association. It has no rule letting adults give cannabis to one another, and it leaves the rest of the drug law in place. Under the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, supplying or distributing a drug is dealing, and payment is not part of the definition. The one concession is in sentencing: where a person shared a small amount to be used on the spot, at a single event and for no profit, the court may relax the stricter sentencing rules for dealing, once only. [9][3]

Dangerous Drugs Ordinance (Cap. 101) art. 22(1B) and (9); Cap. 537 art. 3 · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 4 plants per household

Adults may grow up to four plants at the home address on their identity card. The limit is four for the household, however many people live there. Plants must be out of public view, out of sight and reach of children, and must not cause a smell nuisance to others. Breaking those conditions costs €300 to €500. Growing up to four plants anywhere other than the registered home costs €700 to €1,000. [3]

Cap. 537 art. 7 · checked November 19, 2026

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted 2 micrograms of THC per litre of blood; random roadside tests

It is an offence to drive with any amount of a drug still in your body. For THC, the law allows drivers of private vehicles up to 2 micrograms per litre of blood; that allowance does not apply to drivers of commercial vehicles, buses, coaches or other passenger-carrying vehicles. Since March 10, 2026 police may demand an oral fluid (saliva) test at random, with no suspicion of the driver needed, and a driver who tests positive for THC can be sent for a blood test. A first conviction brings a fine of at least €4,000 and a driving ban of at least six months; a later one, at least €8,000 and at least a year. Holding a drug control card or a prescription does not exempt a driver. [7][16][17]

Traffic Regulation Ordinance (Cap. 65) arts. 15BA, 15CB, 15CC, 15CD and 15H; Act II of 2026; Malta Police · checked November 19, 2026

Can an old cannabis record be cleared?

Allowed Yes, by written request

A person convicted of something that has since been decriminalised can write to the office that issues conduct certificates and ask for the conviction to be taken off. Once the office confirms the offence has been decriminalised, it must accept the request and remove the entry. The government says no new court application is needed. [8][2]

Conduct Certificates Ordinance (Cap. 77) art. 8A; Government of Malta · checked November 19, 2026

Can you take it across the border?

Not permitted No. Importing is a crime, even for your own use

Importing or exporting cannabis without authorisation is an offence, and a court treats importation as dealing unless it is satisfied the drug was only for the person's own use. Even then, the Court of Magistrates can give three to twelve months in prison, a fine of €465.87 to €2,329.37, or both. The law exempts a visitor from criminal liability if they do not live in Malta and, at the first opportunity after landing, hand the drug to a police or customs officer and declare it was for personal use only, in an amount that fits that. A traveller from Spain who arrived with a little cannabis for his holiday, and half a gram of amphetamine, was charged with importation and fined €700. The UK government warns its travellers that drug penalties in Malta can include a ban from the Schengen area of up to five years. [9][18][19]

Dangerous Drugs Ordinance (Cap. 101) arts. 14, 15A and 22; MaltaToday court report (news); UK FCDO travel advice · checked November 19, 2026

Good to know

Parliament tightened the law through Act XII of 2025, in force since May 16, 2025. Synthetic cannabinoids such as HHC and THC-P were prohibited, and letting anyone under 18 into an association now costs €3,000 to €10,000. Separately, ARUC lets associations make and hand out resin, by solventless methods only, with one gram of resin counted as three grams of flower toward a member's limits. Regulations made in February 2026 cap the stock at each distribution site by category, from 350 grams to 3.5 kilograms. On November 5, 2026 the home affairs minister said tourists caught smoking cannabis in public would get a €235 on-the-spot fine; the legal notice was still being drafted. [5][3][20][21][22]
