The law in Vermont

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Adults 21 and older with a valid government-issued photo ID may buy cannabis.

Vermont Cannabis Control Board, Frequently asked questions · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

Vermont has a medical cannabis program.

Vermont Cannabis Control Board · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed retailers, 2 ounces per sale

Cannabis may only be bought legally from a licensed retailer or an integrated licensee’s retail arm. Since July 1, 2026, a retailer may sell up to 2 ounces, or the equivalent in products, in a single transaction to a buyer 21 or older with valid photo ID.

Vermont Cannabis Control Board, Frequently asked questions; 7 V.S.A. § 907(b), as amended by Act 176 (2026) · in effect July 1, 2026 · checked November 15, 2026 [1][3] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 2 ounces or 10 grams of hashish

Since July 1, 2026, adults 21 and older may possess up to 2 ounces of cannabis or 10 grams of hashish. Cannabis grown and stored at home under the home-grow rules does not count toward the limit.

18 V.S.A. § 4230a, as amended by Act 176 (2026) · in effect July 1, 2026 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 1 oz or 5 g hashish, not advertised

An adult 21 or older may dispense up to 1 ounce of cannabis or 5 grams of hashish to another adult 21 or older, as long as it is not advertised or promoted to the public. Unlawful sales are a crime.

18 V.S.A. § 4230(b)(4) · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 2 mature and 4 immature plants

Adults may grow up to two mature and four immature plants, and a dwelling unit may have no more than that however many adults live there. Plants must be in an area screened from public view, with access limited to the grower and adults 21 or older who have the grower’s permission.

18 V.S.A. § 4230e · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted No

Consuming cannabis in any form in a public place is prohibited, with civil penalties of up to $100 for a first offense.

7 V.S.A. § 833; Vermont Cannabis Control Board, Public safety and education questions · checked November 15, 2026 [6][7] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

The adult-use law does not protect anyone from arrest or prosecution for operating a vehicle under the influence of cannabis or for consuming cannabis while operating a motor vehicle.

18 V.S.A. § 4230a(b)(2) · checked November 15, 2026 [8] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Medical patients with a disability

Vermont’s Attorney General said in 2018 guidance that the state’s medical cannabis laws do not let employers discriminate against applicants or employees with disabilities who use medical cannabis outside work to treat the disability, under the Fair Employment Practices Act, which protects even current illegal drug use unless it keeps the person from doing the job or is a direct threat. The adult-use law lets employers ban or regulate cannabis at work and adopt policies against it, and gives no claim to an employee fired under a zero-tolerance policy covering use on or off the clock. Employer drug testing is allowed only in limited circumstances (21 V.S.A. §§ 511 to 519). The guidance dates from June 2018 and has not been checked against later changes in the law.

Vermont Attorney General, Guide to Vermont’s Laws on Marijuana in the Workplace (June 2018) · checked November 15, 2026 [9] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Pre-2021 possession records expunged by Jan. 2022

Vermont’s Act 167 of 2020 directed the Criminal Division of the Superior Court to order the expungement of criminal history records of violations of 18 V.S.A. § 4230(a)(1) committed before January 1, 2021, with no petition, and gave the court and every entity holding such records until January 1, 2022 to finish. The same act made that offense a civil violation from January 1, 2021; as amended it covers possessing more than one ounce of marijuana or five grams of hashish, or growing more than two mature or four immature plants, below the amounts that remain crimes. The court must notify the person and issue a certificate that the offense has been decriminalized; the act sets no fee. Other cannabis convictions go through the general law by petition: a conviction for conduct no longer prohibited can be expunged once the sentence is complete and restitution and surcharges are paid, and qualifying convictions can be sealed three years (misdemeanors) or seven years (felonies, which include drug offenses under § 4230) after the sentence unless the prosecutor shows sealing is against the interests of justice.

Act No. 167 of 2020, Sec. 31; 13 V.S.A. §§ 7601 and 7602 · in effect October 7, 2020 · checked November 16, 2026 [10][11][12] Official source
