How patients get a medical card in North Dakota

From North Dakota Health and Human Services, Medical Marijuana Program. Adults without a card cannot buy here.

Steps

  1. Create an account in the state’s BioTrackTHC registration system.
  2. Submit the patient application with a photo, a copy of your North Dakota ID, your provider’s name and email, and a signed release.
  3. Buy only at one of the state’s eight registered dispensaries.

Steps as the state describes them [1]

Who can get one
North Dakota residents with a written certification from a healthcare provider licensed in North Dakota. Veterans using VA care and hospice patients have a separate approval route. [1]
Cost
$40, non-refundable. [1]
How long it lasts
Renew up to 45 days before your card expires. [1]
Caregivers
Up to five per patient. Caregivers pass a criminal history check. [1]
Visiting patients
Visitors 19 or older with a home-state card can get a 60-day North Dakota card for $40. [2]
Where patients buy
Eight registered dispensaries. [1]

The law in North Dakota

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Medical patients only No

North Dakota has no adult-use law. Without a medical card, possessing less than half an ounce is an infraction. Half an ounce up to 500 grams is a class B misdemeanor, and more than 500 grams is a class A misdemeanor.

N.D.C.C. 19-03.1-23(7)(d) · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes, with a state registry card

North Dakota residents with written certification from a North Dakota-licensed provider can apply for a registry card ($40). Cardholders may buy only from the state’s eight registered dispensaries; state law does not protect marijuana bought anywhere else.

North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Medical Marijuana Patients · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application First possession of 1 oz or less, by motion

North Dakota’s courts say a person convicted of possessing an ounce or less of marijuana may move the court to expunge the record if it was a first offense and, as the guide puts it, there is no other conviction within two years (N.D.C.C. § 19-03.1-23). Other convictions can be sealed by petition under the general law (N.D.C.C. chapter 12-60.1): three years without a new conviction after a misdemeanor, five after a felony, with exceptions for some violent and sex offenses. Sealed records are kept but not disclosed, and criminal history reported to the Bureau of Criminal Investigation cannot be sealed. The courts’ guide also says people convicted of marijuana possession, ingestion or paraphernalia with no conviction in the past five years may apply for a summary pardon, which the Governor signs.

N.D.C.C. § 19-03.1-23 and ch. 12-60.1, per the North Dakota Courts’ research guides (May 2024; August 2025) · checked November 16, 2026 [5][6] Official source
