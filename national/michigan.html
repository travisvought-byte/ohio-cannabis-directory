The law in Michigan

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Michigan law allows personal possession and use of marijuana by people 21 and older.

Michigan Cannabis Regulatory Agency, Consumer connection · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

The Michigan Medical Marijuana Program registers patients and caregivers.

Michigan Cannabis Regulatory Agency, Consumer connection · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed retailers

Licensed marijuana retailers may sell to adults 21 and older. Adults may purchase and possess up to 2.5 ounces, no more than 15 grams of it concentrate.

MCL 333.27960 and 333.27955 · checked November 15, 2026 [2][3] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 2.5 oz (15 g concentrate); 10 oz at home

Adults may possess up to 2.5 ounces, no more than 15 grams of it concentrate. Within their residence they may keep up to 10 ounces, plus marijuana from plants grown there.

MCL 333.27955 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 2.5 ounces, free

Adults may give away up to 2.5 ounces, no more than 15 grams of it concentrate, to another adult 21 or older without payment, as long as the transfer is not advertised or promoted.

MCL 333.27955 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed Up to 12 plants per residence

Adults may grow up to 12 plants for personal use within their residence, with no more than 12 plants on the premises at once.

MCL 333.27955(1)(b) · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted No

The adult-use law does not authorize consuming marijuana in public.

Michigan Cannabis Regulatory Agency, Michigan Regulation and Taxation of Marihuana Act · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

The adult-use law does not authorize operating a vehicle or other machinery under the influence of marijuana.

Michigan Cannabis Regulatory Agency, Michigan Regulation and Taxation of Marihuana Act · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Not protected Employers may enforce workplace drug policies

Michigan’s adult-use law says it does not require an employer to permit or accommodate conduct the act allows in any workplace or on the employer’s property. It does not prevent an employer from refusing to hire, discharging or disciplining a person for violating a workplace drug policy or for working while under the influence of marihuana.

MCL 333.27954(3) · in effect December 6, 2018 · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Clean Slate after 7 years; marijuana by application

Michigan’s Attorney General says the State Police run a daily automatic process under the Clean Slate laws, which cover criminal records in general and are not specific to cannabis: misdemeanors can be set aside after seven years (any number punishable by less than 93 days, up to four of 93 days or more) and up to two felonies after ten years, with no application. Separately, under MCL 780.621e a person convicted of a misdemeanor marijuana offense (possession, use, selling paraphernalia or a matching local ordinance) may apply to the convicting court on form MC 227a; the conduct is presumed to be something that would not have been a crime on or after December 6, 2018, and if the prosecutor does not answer within 60 days the court must set the conviction aside. A set-aside removes the conviction from public view; law enforcement and some licensing agencies may still see it.

MCL 780.621e and 780.621g, per the State Court Administrative Office’s Clean Slate overview and the Department of Attorney General’s Expungement Assistance page · checked November 15, 2026 [6][7] Official source
