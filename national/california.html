The law in California

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Adults 21 and older may buy cannabis for adult use.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes, 18+ with a recommendation

People 18 and older with a physician’s recommendation may buy for medicinal use, and medicinal users have higher possession limits.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed retailers

Every cannabis retailer must hold a Department of Cannabis Control license. The department’s license search finds licensed retailers.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 28.5 grams; 8 grams concentrate

Adults 21 and older may possess up to 28.5 grams of cannabis and up to 8 grams of concentrated cannabis.

Judicial Council of California, Proposition 64: The Adult Use of Marijuana Act · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Same amounts, without compensation

Adults may give away up to 28.5 grams of cannabis and 8 grams of concentrated cannabis to another adult 21 or older without compensation.

Judicial Council of California, Proposition 64: The Adult Use of Marijuana Act · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed Up to 6 plants

Adults 21 and older may grow up to six cannabis plants at home.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted Not in public; private property only

Cannabis may be used on private property, but not in public places such as restaurants or bars.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No open containers while driving

Having an open container of cannabis while driving or riding in a vehicle is illegal. Cannabis in a vehicle must be in a sealed package or container, or in the trunk. Officers can pull a driver over for a sobriety test if they suspect the driver is under the influence of cannabis.

California Department of Cannabis Control, What’s legal · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Protected Off-the-job use, with exceptions

Since January 1, 2024, California employers generally cannot refuse to hire, fire or penalize someone for using cannabis off the job and away from the workplace, or because a drug test finds only non-psychoactive cannabis metabolites. Employers may still act on a scientifically valid test for psychoactive THC, keep a drug-free workplace and bar use, possession or impairment on the job. The protections do not cover employers with four or fewer employees, jobs that need a federal background investigation or security clearance, or employees in the building and construction trades.

Cal. Gov. Code § 12954, per the Civil Rights Department’s FAQ (January 2024) · in effect January 1, 2024 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Offenses Prop. 64 reduced or eliminated

California’s courts say the state reviews records itself: under a 2019 law the Department of Justice identified cases that might qualify under Proposition 64, and a 2023 law required courts to grant relief by March 1, 2023 in cases prosecutors had not challenged. A person can also apply. Someone who has completed a sentence may ask the trial court to dismiss and seal an eligible conviction, or to redesignate it as a misdemeanor or infraction; someone still serving a sentence may petition for resentencing or dismissal.

Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 11361.8 and 11361.9, per the Judicial Branch of California’s Proposition 64 page · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source
