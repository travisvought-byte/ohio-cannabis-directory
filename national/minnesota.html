The law in Minnesota

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

A person 21 or older may possess up to 2 ounces of flower in public and 2 pounds at home, plus 8 grams of concentrate and edibles with up to 800 milligrams of THC in total.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management, Adult-use cannabis · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

The Office of Cannabis Management runs Minnesota’s medical cannabis program.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed adult-use retailers

Adult-use cannabis is available for purchase at any licensed adult-use retailer.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management, Guide to Safer Cannabis Use (June 2026) · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 2 ounces in public, 2 pounds at home

Also 8 grams of concentrate and edibles infused with a combined 800 milligrams of THC or less.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management, Adult-use cannabis · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed 2 oz flower, 8 g concentrate, 800 mg, free

Adults may give, for no remuneration, up to 2 ounces of flower, 8 grams of concentrate, or an edible with up to 800 milligrams of THC to another adult 21 or older.

Minn. Stat. § 342.09, subd. 1(a)(6) · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed Up to 8 plants per home, 4 mature

Plants must be kept in an enclosed, locked space that is not open to public view.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management, Adult-use cannabis · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Limited Banned in listed places

Use and possession are banned at public and charter schools, state correctional facilities, federal property, federally subsidized housing and an employer’s premises, and while driving or operating heavy machinery.

Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management, Adult-use cannabis · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

Driving under the influence of cannabis is illegal and subject to additional DWI sanctions.

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Protected Off-premises use in nonworking hours

An employer may not refuse to hire an applicant, or discipline or discharge an employee, for using lawful consumable products off the employer’s premises during nonworking hours. Cannabis flower, cannabis products and lower-potency hemp edibles count as lawful products under Minnesota law. Employers may still act on use, possession or impairment during working hours, on work premises or while operating the employer’s vehicle or equipment, or where federal or state law requires it or a federal benefit would be lost. Exceptions include bona fide occupational requirements and conflicts of interest.

Minn. Stat. § 181.938 · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Certain misdemeanors; a board reviews felonies

Minnesota’s Bureau of Criminal Apprehension says the Adult-Use Cannabis Act requires automatic expungement of certain earlier cannabis records; it expunged the qualifying records in its system in May 2024 and will review again in 2026. The state’s cannabis record site says these are misdemeanor convictions and certain offenses that did not end in a conviction, that sealed records will not show on most background checks, and that no one is notified: a person can check the courts’ online case search or a courthouse terminal. Felony and gross misdemeanor records go to the Cannabis Expungement Board for review; no application is needed, though a person may apply for earlier consideration. The separate Clean Slate Act, in effect since January 1, 2025, automatically seals certain other records.

Minn. Stat. §§ 609A.055 and 609A.06, per the Bureau of Criminal Apprehension’s Expungements page and the state’s cannabis record FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [6][7] Official source
