The law in New York

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Adults 21 and older may possess, buy and transport up to 3 ounces of cannabis and 24 grams of concentrate. Up to 5 pounds may be kept at home if secured from anyone under 21.

N.Y. Penal Law §§ 222.05 and 222.15; Office of Cannabis Management · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [1][2][3] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

A certifying provider may certify a patient for any condition they judge suitable. Patients or caregivers may buy up to a 60-day supply. A 2025 law (S3294A) lets registered patients and caregivers 18 and older grow at home and lets patients certified in other states buy in New York.

Office of Cannabis Management, Patients; Senate Bill S3294A (2025) · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [4][5] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Yes, up to 3 ounces

Adults 21 and older may buy up to 3 ounces of cannabis and 24 grams of concentrate.

N.Y. Penal Law § 222.05; Office of Cannabis Management · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [1][3] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 3 ounces and 24 g concentrate; 5 pounds at home

Up to 5 pounds may be kept at home if secured from anyone under 21.

N.Y. Penal Law §§ 222.05 and 222.15 · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [1][2] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Same amounts, without payment

Adults may give up to 3 ounces and 24 grams of concentrate to another adult 21 or older without compensation. “Gifting” tied to a sale is not lawful sharing.

N.Y. Penal Law § 222.05 · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [1] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 3 mature and 3 immature plants per adult

No more than six mature and six immature plants per residence, only at a private residence and secured from anyone under 21. Localities may regulate but not effectively ban home growing.

N.Y. Penal Law § 222.15; Office of Cannabis Management, Home cultivation FAQs · in effect June 26, 2024 · checked November 1, 2026 [2][6] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Limited Where tobacco smoking is allowed, with exceptions

Not in a motor vehicle, where smoking or vaping is banned, on school grounds or buses, or on federal property. Breaking these limits carries a civil penalty of up to $25 or up to 20 hours of community service.

N.Y. Penal Law § 222.10; Office of Cannabis Management · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [7][3] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted Impaired driving is a crime

Driving impaired by a drug is treated like alcohol-impaired driving. Cannabis in a vehicle must be in a closed container, and smoking cannabis in a vehicle is illegal for drivers and passengers.

N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1192; Office of Cannabis Management · checked November 1, 2026 [8][3] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Protected Lawful off-duty use, with exceptions

Employers generally may not act against workers for lawful off-duty cannabis use away from the workplace and without employer property. Employers may still act when required by state or federal law, when a worker shows specific articulable signs of impairment on the job, or when allowing it would violate federal law or risk a federal contract or funding.

N.Y. Labor Law § 201-d · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [9] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Listed marijuana convictions are expunged

Convictions under the listed marijuana offenses (possession up to 16 ounces or sale up to 25 grams) are expunged automatically, with no motion or fee and no notice sent. Expunged cases do not appear on background checks and need not be disclosed. Other marijuana convictions require a CPL 440.46-a motion. Separately, the Clean Slate Act, in effect since November 16, 2024, provides automatic sealing of eligible convictions for certain civil background checks; courts have until November 16, 2027 to finish setting it up. Sex crimes and non-drug Class A felonies are not eligible.

N.Y. Unified Court System: cannabis expungement page and Clean Slate Act page · in effect March 31, 2021 · checked November 1, 2026 [10][11] Official source
