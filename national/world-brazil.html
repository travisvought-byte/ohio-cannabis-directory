Cannabis laws in Brazil

Planning a trip to Brazil, or live there? Adults: Not a crime since 2024, but still prohibited · Buying: Only medical products, in pharmacies · Carrying: Up to 40 grams is presumed personal use · At the border: Importing is trafficking; medical import needs Anvisa.

Since a June 2024 Supreme Court ruling, carrying up to 40 grams or growing up to six female plants for your own use is no longer a crime, but cannabis is still prohibited: police seize it and a judge can order a warning or an educational course. There is no legal adult market. Medical products are sold in pharmacies on prescription or imported with approval from Anvisa, the health regulator.

Portuguese sources are summarized in English. Court rulings shape much of this law.

What is different about Brazil

  • The change came from the Supreme Court, not Congress. The 40-gram line applies until Congress passes its own rule.
  • The 40 grams is a starting presumption, not a safe harbour: signs of sale can still lead to a trafficking arrest.
  • Patients who grow at home rely on individual court orders rather than a licence.
  • Nonprofit patient associations have their own route under Anvisa's 2026 rules, without commercial sale.

[1][2][3][4]

Travelling? Never carry cannabis across an international border, even between two places where it is legal. See where it can land you in prison.

Can adults use it without a prescription?

Limited Not a crime since 2024, but still prohibited

In June 2024 the Supreme Federal Court (STF) ruled in RE 635659 (Tema 506) that acquiring, keeping, transporting or carrying cannabis for personal consumption is not a criminal offence. It stays an unlawful act outside criminal law: police seize the cannabis, and a judge can impose a warning about its effects and attendance at an educational programme, in a non-criminal procedure. The ruling covers cannabis only, and there is no legal place to buy it for adult use. [1][2]

STF, RE 635659 (Tema 506), thesis fixed Jun 26 2024; TJMG summary of the thesis · checked November 19, 2026

Is there a medical program?

Allowed Pharmacy products on prescription, or import

Anvisa rule RDC 1.015/2026 replaced RDC 327/2019 and took effect on May 4, 2026. Authorised cannabis products are dispensed by a pharmacist in pharmacies and drugstores. Doctors and dentists can prescribe them when no registered medicine is a satisfactory alternative. Products with up to 0.2% THC need a special control prescription; above 0.2% THC needs a Notificação de Receita A, and those products are limited to serious debilitating or life-threatening conditions. The 2026 rule named fibromyalgia and lupus among the qualifying conditions and added skin, under-the-tongue, cheek and inhaled forms. Separately, a patient can apply to Anvisa to import a product for personal use under RDC 660/2022. [5][4][6]

Anvisa, Q&A on RDC 1.015/2026 (Jul 20 2026), arts. 5, 35-37; Anvisa (Feb 3 2026); CFF (Mar 9 2026) · checked November 19, 2026

Where can adults buy it legally?

Medical patients only Only medical products, in pharmacies

There is no legal adult-use sale. Selling, supplying or delivering drugs, even for free, is trafficking under article 33 of the Drug Law, punished with 5 to 15 years in prison and a fine. Buying for your own use falls under the Supreme Court's non-criminal treatment, but the cannabis is still seized. The legal purchase is a prescribed product from a pharmacy or drugstore. [7][2][5]

Lei 11.343/2006 art. 33; STF Tema 506; Anvisa RDC 1.015/2026 Q&A · checked November 19, 2026

How much can an adult carry?

Limited Up to 40 grams is presumed personal use

Until Congress legislates, anyone with up to 40 grams of cannabis or six female plants is presumed to be a user. The presumption is relative: police can still arrest for trafficking below that amount if there are signs of sale, such as packaging, scales or sales records, but the police chief must justify it in detail and a judge reviews it at the custody hearing. Above 40 grams, a judge can still find personal use if there is enough proof. The sanctions are a warning and an educational programme, with no criminal consequences. [1][2]

STF, RE 635659 (Tema 506), thesis items 1-8; STF news (Jun 26 2024) · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults share it?

Not permitted Sharing is still a crime

Offering cannabis occasionally and without profit to someone you know, so you can use it together, is a crime under article 33, paragraph 3 of the Drug Law: 6 months to 1 year of detention plus a fine. Other supplying, even for free, is trafficking under article 33. The 2024 ruling addresses possession for personal consumption. [7][2]

Lei 11.343/2006 art. 33 and §3; STF Tema 506 · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults grow it at home?

Limited Six female plants presumed personal; medical growers go to court

The Drug Law treats growing a small amount for your own use the same way as possession (article 28, paragraph 1), and the Supreme Court's presumption covers six female plants, so this is handled outside criminal law but is not legal. Patients who grow for treatment have won habeas corpus orders (salvo-conduto) from the Superior Court of Justice case by case, with proof of medical need; the order is limited to the prescription and bars selling or giving away the plants or extracts. Anvisa's February 2026 rules authorise production only by companies after inspection, plus a separate route for nonprofit patient associations that does not allow commercial sale. [7][2][3][4]

Lei 11.343/2006 art. 28 §1; STF Tema 506; STJ (Jun 7 2023); Anvisa (Feb 3 2026) · checked November 19, 2026

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted Driving under the influence is banned

Under article 165 of the Traffic Code, driving under the influence of alcohol or any other psychoactive substance that causes dependence is a very serious violation: a fine of ten times the base amount, a 12-month suspension, the licence taken and the vehicle held. Impairment can be shown by a blood test, a clinical exam or a set of observed signs. Driving with impaired capacity is also a crime under article 306, with 6 months to 3 years of detention. The 2024 ruling did not change these rules. [8][9]

Código de Trânsito Brasileiro arts. 165 and 306; CONTRAN MBFT ficha 516-92; Vrum (news, Sep 2024) · checked November 19, 2026

Can an old cannabis record be cleared?

Limited No criminal record for personal possession

The Supreme Court ruled that sanctions for personal possession are applied with no criminal repercussion. It also told the National Council of Justice, with the Public Defender's Office, to run prison reviews to correct arrests made outside the ruling's limits. No automatic clearing of older convictions was found in the sources reviewed. [1][2]

STF, RE 635659 (Tema 506); STF news (Jun 26 2024) · checked November 19, 2026

Can you take it across the border?

Not permitted Importing is trafficking; medical import needs Anvisa

Importing or exporting drugs without authorisation is trafficking under article 33 of the Drug Law, with 5 to 15 years in prison, and the penalty rises by one sixth to two thirds when the crime crosses national borders (article 40, I). The route for an individual to bring in a cannabis product is an Anvisa authorisation under RDC 660/2022, for personal use with a prescription. [7][5]

Lei 11.343/2006 arts. 33 and 40, I; Anvisa RDC 1.015/2026 Q&A (on RDC 660/2022) · checked November 19, 2026

Good to know

Anvisa's 2026 update opens a possible path for compounding pharmacies to prepare cannabis products, but a specific rule still has to be written and approved before that can happen. [4]
