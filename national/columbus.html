Visiting Columbus? The cannabis rules for visitors

In Columbus for a game, a convention or a weekend? Ohio sells cannabis to adults 21 and over with a government-issued ID, and this site is an Ohio dispensary directory, so the Columbus and Franklin County listings are one tap away. Here is what Ohio’s 2026 law says about limits, where you can use it, cars and flying home, each from the law itself.

Can tourists buy weed in Columbus?

Yes, if you are 21 or older and show a current, valid, government-issued ID at a dispensary licensed in Ohio.

  • Ohio’s law covers adult-use consumers, people 21 and older, who have and use marijuana bought from a dispensary licensed in Ohio. [1][2]
  • A dispensary may sell adult-use marijuana only to a buyer who shows a current, valid, government-issued identification card proving they are 21 or older. The law does not say the ID must be from Ohio. [3]
  • An adult may give another adult 21 or older up to the same amounts in one day, but only for free and only on private property used mainly as a home or farm. Any other sale or transfer takes a license. [1]

Where are the licensed dispensaries in and around Columbus?

Ohio is home turf for this site: the Ohio dispensary finder on OhioWeed.org lists the licensed locations in Columbus and in the rest of Franklin County.

  • Adult-use marijuana has to come from a dispensary licensed in Ohio; marijuana from anywhere else can’t be legally possessed here. [1][2] See the Columbus listings in the Ohio dispensary finder
  • Since March 20, 2026, Ohio caps the number of dispensaries, adult-use and medical together, at 400 statewide. [2] See every Franklin County listing, suburbs included

How much weed can I buy and have in Ohio?

Up to 2.5 ounces of plant material and 15 grams of extract. That is also the most a dispensary may sell you in one day.

  • An adult may have up to 2.5 ounces of plant material, not counting seeds, live plants or clones, and up to 15 grams of extract. The limits count dispensary and homegrown marijuana together. [1]
  • A dispensary may not knowingly sell one consumer more than that amount in a single day. [3]
  • Under the 2026 law, adult-use flower is capped at 35% THC and extracts at 70%, the same caps as medical marijuana. [2]

Where can you smoke weed in Columbus?

Only on private property used mainly as a home or farm, and not where a lease bans it. Ohio bars using cannabis in any public place, and bars smoking or vaping it in public places and workplaces.

  • No one may knowingly consume adult-use or homegrown marijuana in a public place, and whoever runs a public place may not knowingly allow it. [4]
  • Smoking, combusting or vaporizing adult-use or homegrown marijuana is allowed only on privately owned property used primarily for residential or agricultural purposes. [5]
  • Smoking or vaping is also barred in any public place or place of employment, a family child care home, a halfway house and a rental home whose lease prohibits it. Breaking the public-place rule is a minor misdemeanor; a lease ban carries no criminal penalty. [5][2]
  • The Legislative Service Commission’s analysis of the 2026 law says nothing about hotels or lodging; the statute allows smoking and vaping only on privately owned property used mainly as a home or farm. [2][5]
  • Edible products must be kept in their original packaging whenever they are not being used. [5]

Can I have weed in my car in Ohio, or drive after using it?

Keep it sealed in the package it was sold in, or in the trunk once it is opened. Driving under the influence is against the law, and passengers may not smoke or vape while the car is being driven.

  • Adult-use or medical marijuana may ride in a vehicle in its original, unopened package. Once opened, it goes in the trunk or, in a vehicle with no trunk, behind the last upright seat or somewhere the driver and passengers don’t normally sit and the driver can’t easily reach. Paraphernalia follows the same rule. [7]
  • Breaking the storage rule is a minor misdemeanor. A passenger who smokes, combusts or vaporizes marijuana while the vehicle is being operated commits a misdemeanor of the third degree. [2]
  • The adult-use law does not let anyone operate a vehicle, streetcar, trackless trolley, watercraft or aircraft while under the influence of marijuana. [1]
  • Ohio’s OVI law makes it an offense to drive under the influence of a drug of abuse, and also to drive with marijuana or its metabolite in your blood or urine at or above set concentrations. [6] Driving and cannabis in every state

Can I bring weed into Ohio from another state?

No. Ohio’s 2026 law treats marijuana bought outside Ohio’s licensed system, such as at another state’s dispensary, as contraband that can’t be legally possessed in Ohio.

  • The Legislative Service Commission’s analysis of S.B. 56 says marijuana acquired elsewhere, such as from an out-of-state dispensary, cannot be legally possessed under the act, and calls out-of-state marijuana contraband. [2]
  • Ohio’s possession rights cover marijuana bought from a dispensary licensed in Ohio or grown at home under Ohio’s home grow law. [1]

Can I fly home with weed from Columbus?

Not legally. TSA says marijuana remains illegal under federal law, and it refers anything illegal its officers find to law enforcement.

  • TSA says marijuana and some cannabis products, including some CBD oil, remain illegal under federal law unless they have no more than 0.3% THC on a dry-weight basis or are FDA-approved. Its officers don’t search for drugs, but they refer anything illegal they find to law enforcement. [8] Check your home state

What changed in Ohio’s cannabis law in 2026?

Senate Bill 56 took effect March 20, 2026. It merged the adult-use law into Ohio Revised Code chapter 3796, limited smoking and vaping to private homes and farms, set rules for carrying cannabis in a car, made daily purchase limits law and made marijuana from other states illegal to have in Ohio.

  • S.B. 56 of the 136th General Assembly was signed by the governor and took effect March 20, 2026; its operating appropriations took effect December 19, 2025. [9][2]
  • The act repeals the Cannabis Control Law that took effect December 7, 2023 (the former chapter 3780), merges it into Ohio Revised Code chapter 3796 and leaves adult-use and medical marijuana with one regulator, the Division of Cannabis Control. [2][1]
  • It turned the purchase limit into a daily limit, added the vehicle storage rule, raised the penalty for passengers who smoke in a moving vehicle and made out-of-state marijuana contraband. [2]
  • Landlords may now prohibit smoking, vaporizing or combusting marijuana in a residential premises, common areas included, and may prohibit home grow. [2]
