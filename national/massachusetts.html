Is weed legal in Massachusetts?

Short answer: Yes. Weed is legal in Massachusetts for adults 21 and older, and for medical patients.

Checked November 15, 2026 against Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission, Know the laws [1]

If you are 21 or older in Massachusetts, here is the short version. Buy: Licensed stores; visitors 21+ with ID · Carry: 2 ounces carried, 10 at home · Grow at home: 6 plants per adult, 12 per home. How patients get a medical card in Massachusetts

From Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission, Medical Use of Marijuana Program. Adults 21 and older can also buy without a card; see the answers below.

The Commission says medical marijuana is not covered by insurance. [2]

Steps

  1. Get certified by a registered Certifying Health Care Provider.
  2. Have a valid driver’s license or government-issued ID and proof of Massachusetts residency.
  3. Apply online with the PIN the program sends you. Paper applications take considerably longer.

Steps as the state describes them [3]

Who can get one
Massachusetts residents with a qualifying medical condition and a written recommendation from a Certifying Health Care Provider. Patients under 18 need two certifying providers, one a pediatrician or pediatric specialist, and a parent or guardian as caregiver. [3]
Cost
No fee for a registration card. A replacement card is $10, and that fee can be waived for verified financial hardship. Providers set their own fees for certification visits. [3]
Caregivers
You may designate up to two Personal Caregivers when you register. If you name a second, one must be an immediate family member. A caregiver can serve up to five patients. [3]
Where patients buy
At a Medical Marijuana Treatment Center, with your registration card and a valid ID. You can print a temporary card once approved. Patients may possess a 60-day supply, up to 10 ounces. [3]

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Partly protected Medical patients, under a court ruling

The adult-use law says it does not require employers to permit or accommodate marijuana use at work and does not affect their power to enforce workplace policies restricting employees’ use. The Trial Court Law Libraries’ guide summarizes the Supreme Judicial Court’s ruling in Barbuto v. Advantage Sales and Marketing (2017): an employee fired for using medical marijuana off-site, and not before or during work, may sue for handicap discrimination. The medical-use law itself has no employment language. The opinion was summarized from the Law Libraries’ guide; it was not read on a court site.

G.L. c. 94G, § 2(e); Barbuto v. Advantage Sales and Marketing, LLC, 477 Mass. 456 (2017), per the Trial Court Law Libraries · checked November 23, 2026 [7][8] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Amounts that have since been decriminalized

Massachusetts’s courts say a person can ask a judge to expunge, meaning permanently destroy, the court records of marijuana offenses where the amount involved has since been decriminalized (G.L. c. 276, § 100K¼, in effect since November 9, 2022). The petitioner must show the amount was two ounces or less, ten ounces or less inside a primary residence, or six or fewer plants for personal use or twelve or fewer on the premises. The petition is filed with the clerk of the court that heard the case, with a copy to the District Attorney, and the court is to act within 30 days. Separately, the Governor’s pardon forgives state convictions for simple possession before March 13, 2024; the courts’ law library says most people need not act and may request a pardon certificate.

G.L. c. 276, § 100K¼, per the Massachusetts Trial Court’s petition form (11/22) and the Trial Court Law Libraries’ criminal records page · in effect November 9, 2022 · checked November 15, 2026 [9][10] Official source
