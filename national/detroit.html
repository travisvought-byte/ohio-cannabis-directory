Visiting Detroit? The cannabis rules for visitors

In Detroit for a game, a show or a weekend? Adults 21 and over can buy at licensed stores with a photo ID and have up to 2.5 ounces, but not use it in public or in a car, and none of it can cross the border into Canada. Here is what the rules say, each from the agency that sets them.

Can tourists buy weed in Detroit?

Yes, if you are 21 or older. Show a valid driver’s license or government-issued photo ID at a store licensed by Michigan’s Cannabis Regulatory Agency.

  • Under Michigan law, adults 21 and older may possess, use, purchase and transport cannabis within the limits below. [1][2]
  • Before a sale, a licensed retailer must check that the customer has a valid driver’s license or government-issued photo ID showing they are 21 or older. [3]
  • An adult 21 or older may also give up to 2.5 ounces to another adult 21 or older, as long as nothing is paid for it and the transfer is not advertised or promoted. [1]

How do I know a Detroit cannabis store is licensed?

Look it up in the Cannabis Regulatory Agency’s “Verify an Adult-Use Establishment License” search.

  • The Cannabis Regulatory Agency’s search lets you verify an adult-use establishment license. Leaving the search fields blank downloads the agency’s whole list. [4] Check a store in Michigan’s adult-use license search

How much weed can I have in Michigan?

Up to 2.5 ounces, of which no more than 15 grams may be concentrate, and up to 10 ounces within your residence. A store may sell you no more than 2.5 ounces in one transaction.

  • Adults 21 and older may possess, use, purchase or transport up to 2.5 ounces of cannabis, of which no more than 15 grams may be concentrate. [1]
  • Within a person’s residence, the limit is 10 ounces. [1]
  • A licensed retailer may not sell more than 2.5 ounces to a customer in a single transaction, of which no more than 15 grams may be concentrate. [3]

Where can you smoke weed in Detroit?

In private, such as a home where the owner allows it. Not in public places, not in a vehicle on a public road, not on school grounds and not in areas under federal jurisdiction such as airports.

  • Michigan’s law does not allow consuming cannabis in a public place, or smoking it where the owner, occupant or manager of the property prohibits it. [5]
  • The Michigan State Police say cannabis use is legal in private, such as at the home of the user, but not in public places, in areas frequented by children such as schools or school buses, in areas under federal jurisdiction such as airports or government buildings, or while driving. [6]
  • The law also does not allow having or consuming cannabis on the grounds of a preschool, primary or secondary school, in a school bus, or in a correctional facility. [5]
  • Whoever owns, occupies or manages a property may ban or limit cannabis use there, but a lease can’t stop a tenant from lawfully having cannabis or using it by means other than smoking. [5]

Can I smoke weed in my Detroit hotel room?

Only if the hotel allows it. Michigan law lets whoever owns or manages a property ban or limit cannabis use there.

  • A person who owns, occupies or manages property may prohibit or regulate cannabis use on that property, and the law does not allow smoking cannabis where the owner, occupant or manager prohibits it. [5]

Can I use cannabis at a lounge or event in Detroit?

Only at a business with a state license for on-site use, in a city or township that has authorized it.

  • A business that lets people consume cannabis on site at a nonresidential location, and charges for entry, sells goods or services, or requires a membership, needs a designated consumption establishment license or a temporary marijuana event license from the Cannabis Regulatory Agency. [7]
  • Under Michigan law, a designated consumption area is not a public place if the municipality has authorized it and it is closed to anyone under 21. [5]

Can I use cannabis in a car in Michigan, or drive after using it?

No. You can’t use it while driving, passengers can’t smoke it in a vehicle on a public road, and driving under the influence is against the law.

  • Michigan’s law does not allow consuming cannabis while operating a vehicle, or smoking it in the passenger area of a vehicle on a public way. [5]
  • It also does not allow operating a vehicle or a boat while under the influence of cannabis. [5]
  • The Michigan State Police say operating under the influence can bring up to 93 days in jail, a fine of up to $500, a license suspension and six points on your license, with heavier penalties when a minor is in the vehicle. Refusing a chemical test means a license suspension. [6]

Can I take weed across the border to Windsor or anywhere in Canada?

No. The Canada Border Services Agency says it is still illegal to cross the border with cannabis, even a small amount and even with a medical authorization.

  • It is still illegal to cross the border with cannabis, even in small amounts, and even when travelling to or from a place where cannabis is legal. [8]
  • Having a medical authorization for cannabis does not make it legal to bring it across the border. [8]
  • If you have cannabis with you when you arrive in Canada, you must declare it. Failing to declare it can lead to arrest and prosecution. [8]

Can I fly home with weed from Detroit?

Not legally. TSA says cannabis remains illegal under federal law, and the Michigan State Police list airports among the areas under federal jurisdiction where cannabis use is not legal.

  • The Michigan State Police say cannabis use is not legal in areas under federal jurisdiction, such as airports or government buildings. [6]
  • TSA says marijuana and some cannabis products, including some CBD oil, remain illegal under federal law unless they have no more than 0.3% THC on a dry-weight basis or are FDA-approved. Does Michigan accept medical cannabis cards from other states?

Yes, at licensed provisioning centers. Bring a valid registry card from your home state and a photo ID, and stay within the purchase limit.

  • Licensed provisioning centers may sell to a visiting qualifying patient who shows a valid, unexpired registry card or its equivalent from their home state and a valid driver’s license or government-issued photo ID, as long as the sale is within the purchasing limit and the product has been tested, labeled and packaged. [10]

How much of an edible should I take?

Be patient. A Michigan state guide says an edible can take anywhere from 15 minutes to a few hours to take effect, and the high is often more intense and lasts longer.

  • Edibles take longer to kick in, anywhere from 15 minutes to a few hours, and often produce a more intense, longer-lasting high. [11]
  • Store cannabis locked up, such as in a lock box or safe, away from children. For a possible poisoning, call poison control at 1-800-222-1222. [11]
