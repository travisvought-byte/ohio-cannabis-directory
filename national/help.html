Federal law

Medical cannabis under a state license is now Schedule III

A federal final rule dated April 28, 2026 placed FDA-approved marijuana products and medical marijuana products regulated by a state medical marijuana license in Schedule III of the Controlled Substances Act. The rule names medical products, not adult-use products. A separate proceeding on moving marijuana generally from Schedule I to Schedule III had a hearing set for June 29 to July 15, 2026; the DEA page reports no decision.

Checked November 15, 2026 [1]

State laws

NORML state-by-state guide to laws and penalties

NORML (the National Organization for the Reform of Marijuana Laws), an advocacy group, keeps pages on cannabis laws and penalties for every state, DC and federal law. It says the pages are not legal advice and suggests checking state statutes or an attorney.

Checked November 15, 2026 [2][3]

Travel

Flying with cannabis

TSA says marijuana and certain cannabis products, including some CBD oil, remain illegal under federal law unless they contain no more than 0.3 percent THC on a dry-weight basis or are FDA-approved. Screeners do not search for drugs, but they refer any they find to law enforcement.

Checked November 15, 2026 [4]

Patients traveling

Medical cannabis patient travel guide

Americans for Safe Access, a medical cannabis patient advocacy group, publishes a guide for patients traveling between states: state-by-state laws, patients’ rights and where medical cannabis can be obtained. The page says it was last updated in December 2024.

Checked November 15, 2026 [5]

Veterans

VA care and state cannabis programs

Taking part in a state marijuana program does not affect eligibility for VA care, and veterans will not be denied VA benefits because of marijuana use. VA providers discuss marijuana use as part of care, but VA clinicians may not recommend medical marijuana or complete state program forms.

Checked November 15, 2026 [6]

Legal help

Find free civil legal aid

The Legal Services Corporation’s tool finds a local legal aid organization by address or city and links to LawHelp.org for legal information and forms. LSC-funded programs serve low-income people with civil legal problems.

Checked November 15, 2026 [7]

Record clearing

Which states seal records automatically

The Clean Slate Initiative, an advocacy organization for automatic record sealing, lists the states and DC whose laws meet its criteria (14 states and DC on November 15, 2026), with links to each state campaign.

Checked November 15, 2026 [8]

50-state comparison of record relief

The Collateral Consequences Resource Center compares expungement, sealing and other record relief across all 50 states and DC, with a summary for each state. The page says it was updated in July 2024. Read it on the center’s site.

Checked November 15, 2026 [9]

Contact lawmakers

Find your state legislators by address

Open States, now run by Plural, looks up your state legislators from an address or your current location. Its page notes an exception for Montana while that state finishes redistricting.

Checked November 15, 2026 [10]

Health and safety

Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222

A free poison help line, open 24 hours a day in over 100 languages, run by the federal Health Resources and Services Administration.

Checked November 15, 2026 [11]

SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357

Free, confidential treatment referral and information for mental health and substance use, 24 hours a day, in English and Spanish.

Checked November 15, 2026 [12]

Business

National Cannabis Industry Association

A national trade association for the legal cannabis industry. Its members include state-licensed cannabis companies, hemp manufacturers and service providers, and it advocates for national policy changes.

Checked November 15, 2026 [13]
