Cannabis laws around the world

Traveling, or living outside the US? Pick a country for what visitors and residents can legally do there, where to buy if anywhere, and what happens at the border.

The same plain answers as the state pages, for other countries, plus the history of where cannabis comes from. Every answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Cannabis 101

THC, CBD, terpenes, product forms, hash and rosin, reading a label, and safer use.

Where weed comes from

The landraces, the places they grew and the people who kept the tradition: India, the Hindu Kush, the Rif, the Bekaa, southern Africa, Jamaica, Thailand, Mexico and Colombia.

Where weed can put you in prison

Countries with death-penalty or long-prison drug laws, and the strictest US states.

Legal for adults

Canada

Allowed Yes. 18 federally, 19 in most provinces, 21 in Quebec

Legal for adults nationwide since October 17, 2018. Provinces set the age, who sells it and some home-grow rules.

Uruguay

Allowed Yes, for registered citizens and residents

The first country to regulate the whole market, under Law 19.172 of December 2013. Citizens and permanent residents register for one of three routes: grow at home, join a club, or buy at a pharmacy. Tourists are shut out.

Germany

Allowed Yes, 18 and older

Legal for adults since April 1, 2024, without shops. Adults can carry some, grow a few plants at home, or join a non-profit cultivation club.

Malta

Allowed Yes, 18 and older, since December 2021

The first EU country to legalize personal use, in December 2021. There are no shops and no legal route for visitors: adults can carry 7 grams, grow four plants at home, or join a licensed non-profit association if they live in Malta.

Czechia

Allowed Yes, 21 and older, for personal use

Since January 1, 2026, adults over 21 can grow three plants, keep 100 grams at home and carry 25 grams. There are no shops and no clubs, and supplying anyone else is still a crime.

Luxembourg

Limited Yes, at home, from your own plants

Since July 2023 adults may grow up to four plants per household from seed and use cannabis at home. There is no legal shop, and carrying it in public is still an offence, fined for 3 grams or less. Medical cannabis is free from hospital pharmacies for qualifying residents.

Guam

Allowed Yes, 21 and over

Guam, a U.S. territory, legalised medical cannabis in 2014 and adult use in 2019. Adults 21 and over may possess up to an ounce and grow at home, but the licensed market has been slow to open. Federal law still applies, including at the airport.

Northern Mariana Islands

Allowed Yes, 21 and over

The Northern Mariana Islands, a U.S. commonwealth, legalised cannabis for medical and adult use in September 2018, the first U.S. territory to legalise adult use by legislation. Federal law still applies.

U.S. Virgin Islands

Allowed Yes, 21 and over (residents and visitors)

The U.S. Virgin Islands legalised adult-use cannabis on January 18, 2023, alongside medical and sacramental use, under one regulator. Possession is legal for adults 21 and over; the first licensed dispensaries were expected to open by the end of 2026. Federal law still applies.

Tolerated, court-driven or pulled back

Netherlands

Limited Tolerated, not legal

Cannabis is still illegal under the Opium Act, but since the 1970s the state has tolerated small-scale use and sales in licensed coffeeshops. Growing for those shops was never allowed; a state experiment with licensed growers started in 2025.

Spain

Limited Private use is not a crime

Not legal, but private use and private growing are not crimes. Hundreds of private cannabis clubs operate in a legal gray zone that courts and police are tightening.

Portugal

Limited Decriminalized, not legal

Decriminalized all drugs for personal use in 2001. Having cannabis for yourself goes to a health-focused panel, not a court, and since 2023 that holds whatever the amount if personal use is shown. Selling and growing are still crimes.

Switzerland

Limited Illegal, but small amounts are tolerated

Illegal above 1% THC, but up to 10 grams for personal use is not punished and using it is a 100 franc fine. About 10,000 adults buy legally through official city pilot trials, and a law for regulated access is still in committee.

Mexico

Limited Only with a COFEPRIS permit

No legal market. The Supreme Court struck down the total ban on personal use in 2021, but Congress never passed a law, so adults need a permit from COFEPRIS, the federal health regulator, to grow and use their own. Selling is still a crime, and tourists get no protection.

Thailand

Not permitted No. Prescription only

The first country in Asia to decriminalize, in 2022, and the first to pull back. Since June 26, 2025 cannabis flower is a controlled herb, sold only against a prescription. A bill to make cannabis medical-only went to Parliament in September 2026.

South Africa

Limited Yes, in private, since a 2018 court ruling

Adults may use, have and grow cannabis in private because of a 2018 Constitutional Court ruling. Selling it is still a crime, and a 2024 law that would set the rules was signed but has not started. Medicines with THC are reached mostly through special permits.

Jamaica

Limited Decriminalised, not legal

Ganja is still illegal for recreational use, but since April 15, 2015 having two ounces or less is a ticket, not a crime. Households may grow five plants, Rastafarians may use it as a sacrament, and licensed herb houses sell it to people with a medical recommendation.

Colombia

Limited Personal use is not a crime, but not legal either

Carrying a personal dose, 20 grams of marijuana, has not been a crime since a 1994 court ruling, and the law allows up to 20 plants. There is no legal adult market: two attempts to legalise one failed in the Senate in 2023. Medical cannabis is licensed, and dried flower became a medical product in October 2025.

Belgium

Not permitted No. Small amounts are the lowest priority

Illegal, but tolerated in small amounts. Since 2005 an adult with up to 3 grams or one plant and no aggravating circumstances is the lowest prosecution priority. Medical use is limited to approved medicines such as Sativex and prescribed CBD preparations. Drivers face zero tolerance.

Italy

Limited Not a crime, but punished administratively

Possession for personal use is not a crime but brings administrative penalties, such as losing your driving licence or passport for a few months. Italy's highest court has held that growing a few plants at home for yourself is not a crime. Doctors can prescribe medical cannabis, including flower grown by a military pharmaceutical plant.

Brazil

Limited Not a crime since 2024, but still prohibited

Since a June 2024 Supreme Court ruling, carrying up to 40 grams or growing up to six female plants for your own use is no longer a crime, but cannabis is still prohibited: police seize it and a judge can order a warning or an educational course. There is no legal adult market. Medical products are sold in pharmacies on prescription or imported with approval from Anvisa, the health regulator.

Argentina

Limited Protected in private by a court ruling, not by law

In the 2009 Arriola ruling the Supreme Court held that punishing an adult for possessing drugs for personal use, when it puts no one else at risk, is unconstitutional. Congress never changed the drug law, so possession is still written as a crime and courts decide case by case. Registered medical patients can grow up to nine flowering plants through the REPROCANN registry. There is no legal market.

Chile

Limited Private personal use is not punished; public use is

Holding small amounts for your own use soon is exempt from the drug law's criminal penalties, but using or carrying cannabis in public is a minor offence punished with fines, programmes or community work. Since 2023 a doctor's prescription justifies growing cannabis for medical treatment, as long as it is not smoked. There is no legal market.

Peru

Limited Small amounts for immediate use are not punished

Possessing up to 8 grams of marijuana, or 2 grams of its derivatives, for your own immediate use is not punishable under the Penal Code. Selling and growing remain serious crimes. Medical cannabis has been legal since 2017 for patients in the national registry, sold through licensed establishments, and registered patients can form associations licensed to grow for their members.

Ecuador

Limited Use is not a crime; there is no legal market

The 2008 Constitution bars treating drug use as a crime, and possession for personal use is not punishable if it is proven. But the 10-gram threshold for marijuana was repealed in November 2023, and since June 2025 personal use is never presumed: prosecutors and judges decide case by case, with toxicology or psychological exams. Only cannabis under 1% THC is outside drug control, grown and sold under licence.

Paraguay

Limited Small personal amounts are exempt; no legal market

Under the 1988 drug law, possessing up to 10 grams of marijuana for personal use is exempt from punishment; more than that can be prosecuted. Growing is still a crime, even a couple of plants. Medical cannabis runs through a state programme created in 2017, with licensed producers, a patient registry and free oil for registered patients.

Costa Rica

Limited Using is not a crime; there is no legal market

Using drugs for yourself is not a crime in Costa Rica, but there is no legal market, and possession that looks like dealing carries at least 8 years. A 2022 law allows psychoactive cannabis only for medical and therapeutic use, and hemp for food and industry.

India

Limited No, except bhang where a state allows it

India bans cannabis flowers (ganja) and resin (charas) under the 1985 NDPS Act, with penalties scaled by quantity. The leaves and seeds are outside the federal definition, so bhang is left to the states: some license bhang shops, others ban it.

Antigua and Barbuda

Limited Decriminalised in private; no recreational sale

Since March 2018, having up to 15 grams of cannabis is not an offence, a household may grow up to four plants, and old convictions for 15 grams or less were expunged. Smoking in public is still an offence and there is no legal recreational sale. A licensed medical system and registered religious use, mainly by Rastafari, run under the Cannabis Act.

Saint Kitts and Nevis

Limited Decriminalised, with an optional adult licence

Small amounts and up to five plants carry no criminal sanction. Adults can also take out a licence under the Freedom of Conscience (Cannabis) Act 2023 to carry up to 56 grams in permitted public places, smoke in designated smoking areas and grow up to five plants at home; fees are waived until the end of 2026. Rastafari sacramental use is recognised in law, and a Medicinal Cannabis Authority licenses sales.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

Limited Decriminalised at home; not legal

Possession of up to 56 grams (two ounces) is still illegal but only a ticketable offence: no arrest and no criminal record. Smoking at home and at Rastafari places of worship is not punished, but smoking in public is unlawful. A Medicinal Cannabis Authority set up in 2019 licenses a medical industry, including dispensaries and a consumption lounge for card holders. The government rejects full legalisation.

Trinidad and Tobago

Limited Decriminalised in private; no legal market

Under a 2019 law, having up to 30 grams of cannabis or 5 grams of resin, and growing up to four plants, is not an offence. Larger amounts and smoking in public carry fines, often settled with a fixed penalty ticket, and old small-possession records were ordered expunged. A Cannabis Control Act for medical and religious use was passed in 2022. A bill introduced in September 2026 would limit the personal-use exemption to people over 25.

Grenada

Limited Decriminalised; no legal sales yet

Since February 2026, possessing up to 56 grams of cannabis or 15 grams of resin is not an offence, and up to four plants per household are allowed. Under-21s found with a personal amount are referred to counselling instead of being charged. Smoking near public places is a ticketable offence and near schools a serious one. Old convictions for these amounts are expunged. There is no legal market yet; the government must bring in a separate law for a regulated industry.

Saint Lucia

Limited Decriminalised in private; no legal market

Since September 2021, possessing 30 grams or less of cannabis or resin is not an offence, and a person may grow up to four plants at their home. Smoking in public is still an offence with a fine of up to $1,500 that does not go on a criminal record. There is no legal market yet; a draft Cannabis and Industrial Hemp Bill went to public consultation in 2025.

Dominica

Limited Small amounts decriminalised; no legal market

Since an October 2020 amendment to the Drugs (Prevention of Misuse) Act, adults 18 and over are not guilty of an offence for having 28 grams or less of cannabis; more than that is still unlawful. There is no legal market, and a medical cannabis framework was still being developed in 2025.

Belize

Limited Allowed in private for 10 grams or less

Since November 2017, possessing 10 grams or less of cannabis is not an offence, except on school premises, and smoking that amount is allowed at your own home, someone else's home with their consent, or a hotel room with the manager's consent. Anything beyond that is still under the Misuse of Drugs Act, and there is no legal market.

Anguilla

Not permitted Still illegal; police policy limits arrests

Cannabis is still illegal in Anguilla, but from December 2024 police stopped enforcing criminal penalties against adults 18 and over with up to 10 grams or up to four plants, used in private. That one-year pilot ended on December 1, 2025; news reports say the same police guidelines stay in place while the police review it. Sales and trafficking are still prosecuted.

Aruba

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Aruba: its narcotics ordinance bans growing, possessing, using, selling and importing it. Under a 2021 prosecution directive, a case of possessing up to 5 grams is normally dismissed once the cannabis is given up, unless there are aggravating factors. Larger amounts are prosecuted.

Curaçao

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Curaçao. Its Opium Ordinance 2024 bans growing cannabis and bans possessing, selling, supplying, importing and exporting it, with no small-quantity threshold in the law. Under prosecution guidelines, up to 25 grams is normally seized and the case dismissed. Curaçao is part of the Kingdom of the Netherlands but has its own drug law, separate from the Dutch one.

St Maarten

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Sint Maarten, the Dutch side of the island. Under prosecution guidelines, up to 25 grams is normally seized and the case dismissed, but growing and selling are prosecuted. The government set up a committee in 2024 to work toward legalisation, and in February 2026 a draft law was still with the Department of Legal Affairs and had not reached Parliament.

Bonaire, St Eustatius and Saba

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal on Bonaire, St Eustatius and Saba. These islands are special municipalities of the Netherlands, but they have their own drug law, the Opiumwet 1960 BES, not the Dutch Opium Act. Under prosecution guidelines, up to 25 grams is normally seized and the case dismissed.

Guyana

Not permitted Illegal, but no jail for small amounts

Cannabis is illegal in Guyana, but since a 2022 amendment there is no prison sentence for possessing 30 grams or less. Up to 15 grams brings mandatory counselling and 15 to 30 grams community service; it remains a criminal offence. Above 30 grams a person must prove they were not trafficking.

Medical programs, adult use illegal

Israel

Not permitted Illegal, but small amounts are a fine, not a crime

One of the world's largest medical programs per person, with about 140,000 licence holders in 2024. Recreational use is illegal, but personal amounts of up to 15 grams are handled with fines.

United Kingdom

Not permitted No. Cannabis is a Class B drug

Illegal for recreational use. Cannabis is a Class B drug. Specialist doctors have been able to prescribe cannabis-based medicines since November 1, 2018, mostly through private clinics.

Australia

Varies locally Only in the Australian Capital Territory

Adult use is illegal almost everywhere. The exception is the Australian Capital Territory, where adults may keep up to 50 grams and grow two plants at home, though nothing can be sold or shared. Medicinal cannabis is prescribed nationwide and dispensed by pharmacies.

New Zealand

Not permitted No. A 2020 vote to legalise it failed narrowly

Illegal for adult use. A 2020 referendum to legalise it failed by about two points. Possession is a crime on paper, but police are told to weigh a health approach and rarely charge it on its own. Medicinal cannabis has been available on prescription since April 2020.

Ireland

Not permitted No. Possession is a crime

Illegal for recreational use. Since December 2020 gardaí can caution a first-time adult found with cannabis for personal use instead of prosecuting. Specialist doctors can prescribe it for three conditions, and a 2026 Oireachtas committee called for decriminalising personal possession.

France

Not permitted No. Use is a crime

Illegal, with no distinction between cannabis and other drugs. Police can settle simple use of cannabis by an adult with a €200 on-the-spot criminal fine. A medical cannabis experiment closed to new patients at the end of 2024 while the government prepares to make it permanent.

Austria

Not permitted No. Possession is a crime under the drug law

Illegal, but when cannabis was only for personal use, the drug law requires prosecutors to set the case aside on probation. Doctors can prescribe cannabinoid medicines such as dronabinol, but not cannabis flower.

Denmark

Not permitted No. Possession is an offence

Illegal. Possession for your own use is normally a fine, starting at 2,000 kroner for under 10 grams. Fines double in designated penalty zones. Medical cannabis, including dried flower, has been prescribed since 2018, and the scheme became permanent on January 1, 2026.

Morocco

Not permitted No. Only medical and industrial uses are legal

Morocco legalised growing and processing cannabis for medical, pharmaceutical and industrial use in 2021, under a national licensing agency. Recreational use, possession and unlicensed growing are still crimes, with severe penalties.

Lebanon

Not permitted No. Use and possession are crimes

Lebanon passed a law in 2020 to license cannabis growing for medical and industrial use, but it has been slow to start. Using and possessing cannabis remain crimes under the 1998 drug law, which lets a court send a user to treatment instead of prison.

Japan

Not permitted No. Using it is a crime since December 2024

Japan made using cannabis a crime on December 12, 2024, when it reclassified cannabis and THC as narcotics. Possession, use and transfer each carry up to 7 years in prison. The same reform allows approved cannabis-derived medicines.

Nepal

Not permitted No

Cannabis has been illegal in Nepal since the Narcotic Drugs (Control) Act of 1976, and even small amounts can mean years in prison. In 2026 Gandaki province passed its own law to license medical and industrial cultivation, and the Supreme Court ordered the federal government to write cultivation rules.

Singapore

Not permitted No. Zero tolerance

Singapore has zero tolerance for cannabis. Consumption alone carries up to 10 years in prison, drug use detected on arrival can mean arrest, and trafficking can bring the death penalty. Only approved cannabinoid medicines, case by case, are allowed.

Malaysia

Not permitted No

Malaysia has some of the world's strictest drug laws, with long prison terms, caning and the death penalty. Since 2021 the Health Ministry has said registered cannabis-based medicines can be prescribed, but none is known to be widely available.

Philippines

Not permitted No

Cannabis is illegal in the Philippines under the Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act (RA 9165), and even a small amount means a mandatory prison sentence. The only medical route is a special permit from the Food and Drug Administration; bills to create a medical program were advancing in 2026.

Poland

Not permitted No

Possession of any amount of cannabis is a crime in Poland, though prosecutors may drop small personal-use cases. Medical cannabis has been available on prescription from pharmacies since 2017.

Greece

Not permitted No

Recreational cannabis is illegal in Greece, but the 2013 drug law treats personal use more leniently than dealing. Licensed medical cannabis production has been legal since 2018, and prescription cannabis became available in pharmacies in 2024.

Norway

Not permitted No

Cannabis is illegal in Norway, but small personal amounts usually bring a fine. Two cannabis medicines are approved, and doctors can apply for special permits for others.

Sweden

Not permitted No

Sweden prohibits cannabis even in small amounts and enforces minor drug offences more than its Nordic neighbours. A few cannabis medicines are available on prescription or under licence.

Finland

Not permitted No

Using, possessing and growing cannabis are crimes in Finland under the Criminal Code. Medical cannabis is available only as Sativex or through individual special permits from the medicines agency.

Puerto Rico

Not yet reviewed

Puerto Rico, a U.S. territory, has had a medical cannabis program since 2015, run by its Department of Health. This guide has not yet reviewed the rules for adult use or possession there. Federal law still applies.

Barbados

Not permitted Illegal; small amounts get a ticket

Cannabis is still illegal for adult use. Since a 2021 amendment, police can hand someone with 14 grams or less a $200 fixed penalty ticket instead of charging them, and older convictions for that amount are treated as expunged. Medical cannabis is legal on a doctor's prescription, filled by a pharmacist; the first licensed dispensary opened in 2025. Rastafari can use cannabis as a sacrament only in permitted places of worship.

Bahamas

Not permitted Recreational use stays illegal

Recreational cannabis is illegal. The Cannabis Act passed in 2024 sets a $250 fine instead of a criminal charge for up to 30 grams and legalises medical and Rastafari religious use, but it has been brought in slowly: arrests for small amounts were still reported in 2025, and the medical licensing portal had not opened as of July 2026.

Bermuda

Not permitted Decriminalised possession only; use is illegal

Since December 20, 2017, simple possession of 7 grams or less of cannabis is not a criminal offence, but cannabis is still a controlled drug: police can seize any amount, and smoking, growing, selling and importing remain illegal. Bermuda's 2022 bill for a licensed cannabis market was blocked when the UK instructed the Governor not to give assent.

Cayman Islands

Not permitted Illegal; reform under consultation

Cannabis is illegal except for prescribed medical extracts and tinctures. Possession and use still carry prison terms on the books. Voters backed decriminalising small amounts in an April 2025 referendum, and the Law Reform Commission was taking public submissions on how to do it until September 1, 2026.

British Virgin Islands

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal. The territory's House of Assembly passed a medical Cannabis Licensing Act and a bill to decriminalise small amounts in 2020, but neither had received the Governor's assent as of July 2026. The UK Home Office remains the licensing authority for narcotics, including medical ones.

Turks and Caicos Islands

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in the Turks and Caicos Islands, with no decriminalised amount, no medical program and no exception for visitors. The UK government warns of severe penalties for possession of even a small amount, and Border Force lists cannabis-infused products and CBD oils among the controlled drugs that are an offence to import.

Montserrat

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Montserrat. In July 2024 the Legislative Assembly passed an amendment that lets magistrates dismiss small possession cases, cuts fines for up to 30 grams and allows doctor-approved medical cannabinoid imports, but it is not confirmed that the amendment has received assent or come into force. In February 2025 the police commissioner said cannabis was still illegal and visitors could be detained.

Dominican Republic

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in the Dominican Republic, with no medical program. Under Law 50-88, up to 20 grams counts as simple possession, which carries 6 months to 2 years in prison and a fine; more than 20 grams is treated as distribution, and more than a pound as trafficking. Growing even a few plants is a prison offence.

Cuba

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Cuba. Under the 2022 Penal Code, possessing marijuana without authorisation or a prescription carries 6 months to 2 years in prison, a fine, or both, and growing cannabis or knowingly holding its seeds carries 4 to 10 years. The UK government describes a zero-tolerance policy on drugs.

Haiti

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Haiti. Drug offences fall under a 2001 drug-control law, and the UK government warns that penalties for possession, use or trafficking are severe and that people can be held for weeks or months before a court date. New Penal and Criminal Procedure Codes are due to take effect in January 2027.

Suriname

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Suriname. The Narcotic Drugs Act bans growing, possessing, using, selling and importing it, with no small-quantity threshold. Since a 2018 amendment, cannabis with no more than 1.5% THC is outside the possession ban, and growing it for industrial purposes is allowed on conditions still to be set by law.

Venezuela

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Venezuela. Under the 2010 Organic Drug Law, a consumer found with a personal dose is put through "social security measures" such as reintegration, follow-up and community service rather than prison. Possession of up to 20 grams for other purposes carries 1 to 2 years, and growing cannabis is treated as trafficking.

Bolivia

Not permitted Illegal; users are sent to treatment

Cannabis is illegal in Bolivia, where Law 1008 prohibits its consumption. A user caught with a minimal amount is sent to a drug-dependence institute for treatment rather than prison, but anyone over that amount, judged as about 48 hours of use by two medical experts, is prosecuted for trafficking, which carries 10 to 25 years.

Panama

Not permitted Illegal

Recreational cannabis is illegal in Panama, but possessing a small amount for personal use is punished with day-fines, weekend arrest or community work rather than prison. Since Law 242 of 2021 Panama allows medical cannabis products; after slow rule-making, licensed companies began selling prescription CBD products through authorised pharmacies in 2026.

Guatemala

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Guatemala, and possessing it for your own use is a crime: 4 months to 2 years in prison and a fine under the 1992 Law against Narco-activity. There is no gram limit; the judge decides whether the amount was reasonable for immediate personal use. Growing carries 5 to 20 years.

El Salvador

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in El Salvador, and even less than 2 grams can mean prison. Under the 2003 drug law, possessing under 2 grams carries 1 to 3 years, 2 grams or more 3 to 6 years, and possession meant for trafficking 6 to 10 years. Growing carries 5 to 15 years.

Honduras

Not permitted Illegal; users face internment

Cannabis is illegal in Honduras. Using it or holding a personal dose leads to compulsory internment and a fine under the 1989 drug law, with the amount set by a forensic doctor. Under the Penal Code in force since 2020, growing or trafficking cannabis carries 4 to 7 years, lower than for other drugs because cannabis derivatives are classed as not causing serious harm to health.

Nicaragua

Not permitted Illegal

Cannabis is illegal in Nicaragua. Under the Penal Code as reformed in September 2024, possessing more than 5 and less than 20 grams of marijuana, or one to five plants, carries 6 months to 3 years in prison; larger amounts carry 3 to 8 years. Drug cases are tried with the accused held in pre-trial detention until sentence.

Banned, with severe penalties

United Arab Emirates

Not permitted No. Zero tolerance

The UAE has zero tolerance for drugs. Possessing even the smallest amount of cannabis can mean at least 3 months in prison or a heavy fine, traces in your blood count as possession, and some CBD products are illegal.

Indonesia

Not permitted No. Zero tolerance

Indonesia has zero tolerance for drugs. Possessing, using, making or trafficking cannabis can bring long prison sentences or the death penalty, and airports scan transit passengers' bags.

Saudi Arabia

Not permitted No. Zero tolerance

Saudi Arabia has zero tolerance for drugs. Possessing even a small amount can mean up to 5 years in prison and a large fine, and dealing or smuggling can bring a death sentence.

China

Not permitted No. Zero tolerance

China has zero tolerance for drugs, including cannabis. People caught face heavy fines, long prison sentences or the death penalty, and airports scan for cannabis.

Türkiye

Not permitted No

Cannabis is illegal in Türkiye. The UK government warns of 4 to 24 years in prison and heavy fines for possessing, using or smuggling drugs, including when transiting through airports.
