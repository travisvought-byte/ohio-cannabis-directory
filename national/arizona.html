The law in Arizona

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

An adult 21 or older may possess and use 1 ounce or less of marijuana, of which no more than 5 grams may be concentrate.

A.R.S. § 36-2852 · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

The Arizona Department of Health Services licenses the medical marijuana program for qualifying patients, caregivers and dispensaries.

Arizona Department of Health Services, Marijuana licensing · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed marijuana establishments

Licensed marijuana establishments may sell to consumers, up to 1 ounce per transaction, no more than 5 grams of it concentrate.

A.R.S. § 36-2858 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 1 ounce (up to 5 g concentrate)

No more than 5 grams of the 1 ounce may be marijuana concentrate.

A.R.S. § 36-2852 · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 1 ounce or 6 plants, free

An adult may transfer 1 ounce or less, or up to six plants, to another adult 21 or older without payment and without advertising or promoting it.

A.R.S. § 36-2852 · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 6 plants per adult, 12 per home

Plants must be at the adult’s primary residence, in an enclosed area with a lock that keeps out minors and not visible from public view. No more than 12 plants at a residence with two or more adults.

A.R.S. § 36-2852 · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Limited No smoking in public places or open spaces

Smoking marijuana in a public place or open space is not allowed and is a petty offense. Consuming it while driving or riding in a vehicle is also not allowed.

A.R.S. §§ 36-2851 and 36-2853 · checked November 15, 2026 [4][5] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving impaired to the slightest degree

The law does not allow driving while impaired to even the slightest degree by marijuana, or consuming it while driving or riding in a vehicle. Metabolites in a driver’s system count toward an impaired-driving charge only if the driver is impaired to the slightest degree.

A.R.S. §§ 36-2851 and 36-2852 · checked November 15, 2026 [4][1] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Medical cardholders only

Arizona law bars an employer from discriminating in hiring, firing or the terms of a job, or otherwise penalizing a person, because the person is a medical marijuana cardholder, or because a registered qualifying patient tested positive for marijuana, unless the patient used, possessed or was impaired by marijuana at the workplace or during work hours. The bar does not apply where it would cost the employer a monetary or licensing benefit under federal law. The adult-use law gives no such protection: it does not restrict an employer’s right to keep a drug-and-alcohol-free workplace or to have policies restricting marijuana use by employees or applicants, and does not require an employer to allow or accommodate use at work.

A.R.S. § 36-2813(B) and § 36-2851 · checked November 15, 2026 [6][4] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application 2.5 ounces or less, six plants or paraphernalia

Arizona’s courts say Proposition 207 added A.R.S. § 36-2862, which lets some people petition a court for an order sealing their marijuana-related criminal records; the courts’ forms call this expungement. It covers a person arrested, charged, convicted or acquitted, for conduct before November 30, 2020, of possessing, consuming or transporting 2.5 ounces or less of marijuana (no more than 12.5 grams of it concentrate), of possessing, transporting, growing or processing up to six plants at a primary residence for personal use, or of a marijuana paraphernalia offense. Courts have taken petitions since July 12, 2021 and may not charge a filing fee. The petition goes to the court that resolved the case or, if no case was filed, to the Superior Court in the county of the arrest. A prosecuting agency may also file.

A.R.S. § 36-2862, per the Arizona Judicial Branch’s Prop 207 page, petition instructions and court staff presentation · in effect July 12, 2021 · checked November 15, 2026 [7][8][9] Official source
