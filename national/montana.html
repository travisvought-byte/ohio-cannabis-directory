The law in Montana

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Adults 21 and older with ID may buy from licensed dispensaries in counties where adult sales are allowed (green counties). Possession limits are 1 ounce of flower, 8 grams of concentrate and 800 milligrams of THC in products. Using marijuana in a public place is prohibited.

Montana Department of Revenue, adult-use consumer summary · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes, with a registry card

Montana residents with a Montana physician’s recommendation can get a one-year registry card for $20 and buy up to 1 ounce a day and 5 ounces a month at any licensed dispensary.

Montana Department of Revenue, cardholder information · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 1 ounce, free

Adults may transfer up to 1 ounce of usable marijuana to another adult 21 or older, or to a registered cardholder, without consideration. No more than 8 grams may be concentrate and no more than 800 milligrams of THC may be in solid edibles.

MCA 16-12-106(1)(b) · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 2 mature plants and 2 seedlings

Adults 21 and older may grow up to two mature plants and two seedlings at a private residence, and no more than twice that at one residence. Plants, and any harvest over 1 ounce, must be kept in a locked space out of public view, and a grower who does not own the home needs the owner’s written permission. The Department of Revenue describes home growing as open to Montana residents.

MCA 16-12-106(1)(c); Montana Department of Revenue, Know before you grow · checked November 15, 2026 [3][4] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted No

State law does not permit consuming marijuana in a public place, except as allowed by the department, or on public transportation. Smoking it in a public place can bring a civil fine of up to $50.

MCA 16-12-108(1)(k) and 16-12-106(4); Montana Department of Revenue, adult-use consumer summary · checked November 15, 2026 [5][3][1] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

State law does not permit operating a vehicle under the influence of marijuana, consuming it while operating a vehicle, or smoking or consuming it in the passenger seat of an enclosed vehicle while it is being operated.

MCA 16-12-108(1)(a) to (c) · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Protected Off-premises use in nonworking hours

Marijuana is a lawful product under Montana’s lawful-product law. An employer may not refuse to employ someone, or discriminate in pay, promotion or other terms of employment, because the person legally uses a lawful product off the employer’s premises during nonworking hours. The rule does not apply to use that affects job performance or the safety of other employees, or that conflicts with a bona fide occupational qualification. An employer also does not violate it by acting in the belief that an established substance abuse or alcohol policy, a professional contract or a collective bargaining agreement permits the action.

Mont. Code Ann. § 39-2-313 (2025) · checked November 15, 2026 [6] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Acts the marijuana law now permits

A person who has completed a sentence for an act that Montana’s marijuana law now permits, or punishes less severely, may petition the sentencing court to expunge the conviction or to redesignate it as a misdemeanor or civil infraction. The court presumes the person qualifies unless the county attorney gives a reasonable basis otherwise, and no hearing is needed unless the person asks for one. A person still serving a sentence may petition for expungement or resentencing. Filing does not affect a person’s separate right to petition for misdemeanor expungement.

Mont. Code Ann. § 16-12-113 (2025) · checked November 15, 2026 [7] Official source
