The law in Arkansas

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Medical patients only No

Arkansas has no adult-use law. Possessing less than 4 ounces of marijuana is a Class A misdemeanor. That is the law as the Arkansas Sentencing Commission quoted it in 2019; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026.

Ark. Code 5-64-419(b)(5)(A), per the Arkansas Sentencing Commission (2019); Ballotpedia; DISA national map · checked November 15, 2026 [1][2][3] Official source and national compilation

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

The Arkansas Department of Health issues medical marijuana registry cards. A separate state agency licenses dispensaries.

Arkansas Department of Health, Medical marijuana · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Medical patients and caregivers only

Arkansas’s constitution, as amended by the legislature in 2017, bars employers from discriminating in hiring, firing or the terms of employment, or otherwise penalizing a person, for being or having been a qualifying patient or designated caregiver. Employers may still keep a drug-free workplace policy with drug testing and act under it, act on a good-faith belief that a patient used marijuana or was under its influence at work or during work hours (a positive test alone cannot be the basis for that belief), and exclude a patient from a safety-sensitive position on a good-faith belief of current use. Damages are limited to those under the Arkansas Civil Rights Act of 1993, and a claim must be filed within a year. A 2021 bill changed the definition of a safety-sensitive position; the current wording was not checked.

Ark. Const. amend. 98, § 3(f)(3), as amended by Act 593 of 2017 · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Court petition under the 2013 sealing act

Arkansas seals records through the court where the person was sentenced, using uniform petitions under the Comprehensive Criminal Record Sealing Act (Act 1460 of 2013). The Arkansas Crime Information Center posts separate petitions for misdemeanors, felonies, convictions for possession of a controlled substance, dismissals and acquittals, arrests and pardoned offenses, and for first offenders under Act 346 of 1975. Once a judge signs the order, the clerk sends it to the Center, which has 30 days to update its records. The page describes no automatic sealing, lists no fee and does not mention marijuana; the law covers criminal records in general.

Act 1460 of 2013 (Ark. Code § 16-90-1401 et seq.), per the Arkansas Crime Information Center’s forms page · checked November 16, 2026 [6] Official source
