The law in Missouri

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Consumers at least 21 years old may possess up to 3 ounces of marijuana.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

Missouri issues medical marijuana ID cards to patients.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Cannabis regulation · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed dispensaries, with ID

Consumers must show a valid government-issued photo ID to buy adult-use products at a dispensary.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 3 ounces

Consumers may possess up to 3 ounces at any time.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 3 ounces, free

Adults may deliver or distribute up to 3 ounces of dried, unprocessed marijuana, or its equivalent, without consideration. Giving marijuana to anyone under 21 is not allowed.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Limited With a state card; 6 flowering plants

Consumers need a personal cultivation identification card, valid for 12 months and renewable. A cardholder may grow six flowering plants, six nonflowering plants over 14 inches and six under 14 inches, 18 in all. Flowering plants must be labeled with the consumer’s name. The plants, and any harvest beyond 3 ounces, must be kept in an enclosed, locked facility at a private residence.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs; Cannabis cultivation fact sheet · checked November 15, 2026 [1][4] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted Not in public places

The constitution does not allow consuming marijuana in a public place outside a licensed area, or smoking it where tobacco smoking is prohibited.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

The adult-use section of the constitution does not allow operating a vehicle under the influence of marijuana, consuming it while operating one, or smoking it in a vehicle that is being operated. A conviction requires evidence that the driver was in fact under the influence, not just THC or its metabolites in the system.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Medical patients; adult-use section gives none

Missouri’s Department of Health and Senior Services says Article XIV of the state constitution, sections 1 and 2, provides certain employment-related protections for patients and consumers, and that employment questions are outside its authority. Section 2, the adult-use section, gives employees and applicants no protection: it does not require an employer to permit or accommodate the conduct it allows, and does not stop an employer from refusing to hire, firing or disciplining someone for working while under the influence of marijuana. The Marijuana Policy Project, an advocacy group, lists protections for medical patients in section 1 covering lawful use off the premises in nonworking hours and positive drug tests, with exceptions for impairment at work, loss of a federal benefit and jobs where use affects performance or safety; section 1 was not read, so that part is not yet checked against the state’s own text.

Mo. Const. art. XIV, § 2, subsection 3(3); Department of Health and Senior Services’ FAQ; Marijuana Policy Project’s chart of state medical cannabis employment protections · checked November 15, 2026 [3][1][5] Official source and national compilation

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Offenses that are no longer crimes

Missouri’s Department of Health and Senior Services says the law lets people with certain non-violent marijuana offenses petition for release from incarceration, parole or probation and to have their records expunged, and that sales-tax revenue pays the government bodies that carry out expungement of certain marijuana offenses. The Marijuana Policy Project, an advocacy group, lists Amendment 3 as directing courts to expunge offenses that are no longer crimes without a petition, misdemeanors by June 8, 2023 and felonies by December 8, 2023; that part is not yet checked against the state’s own text.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs; Marijuana Policy Project’s table of legalization states’ expungement laws · checked November 15, 2026 [1][6] Official source and national compilation
