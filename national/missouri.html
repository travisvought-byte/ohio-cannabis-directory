How patients get a medical card in Missouri

From Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services. Adults 21 and older can also buy without a card; see the answers below.

Steps

  1. Have an MD, DO or nurse practitioner complete the Physician Certification Form.
  2. Register in the department’s secure online registry portal and submit the patient application.
  3. Renew in the window from 60 to 30 days before the card expires, with a new certification.

Steps as the state describes them [1]

Who can get one
Patients with a qualifying condition certified by an MD, DO or nurse practitioner licensed and in good standing in Missouri. [2]
How long it lasts
Three years for applications approved on or after December 8, 2022. [2]
Caregivers
Caregivers apply online with a Patient Authorization Form and the patient’s ID card number. A minor’s parent or legal guardian must apply as caregiver. [1]
Visiting patients
A person with an equivalent card or authorization from another state is not subject to arrest under Missouri law for possessing less than the limits set for Missouri patients. [2]
Where patients buy
Licensed dispensaries. A patient may buy up to 6 ounces in 30 days unless certified for more. [2]

Not stated on the state pages we checked: cost. Check with the state.

The law in Missouri

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

Consumers at least 21 years old may possess up to 3 ounces of marijuana.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes

Missouri issues medical marijuana ID cards to patients.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Cannabis regulation · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Allowed Licensed dispensaries, with ID

Consumers must show a valid government-issued photo ID to buy adult-use products at a dispensary.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Allowed 3 ounces

Consumers may possess up to 3 ounces at any time.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Up to 3 ounces, free

Adults may deliver or distribute up to 3 ounces of dried, unprocessed marijuana, or its equivalent, without consideration. Giving marijuana to anyone under 21 is not allowed.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [6] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Limited With a state card; 6 flowering plants

Consumers need a personal cultivation identification card, valid for 12 months and renewable. A cardholder may grow six flowering plants, six nonflowering plants over 14 inches and six under 14 inches, 18 in all. Flowering plants must be labeled with the consumer’s name. The plants, and any harvest beyond 3 ounces, must be kept in an enclosed, locked facility at a private residence.

Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs; Cannabis cultivation fact sheet · checked November 15, 2026 [4][7] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Not permitted Not in public places

The constitution does not allow consuming marijuana in a public place outside a licensed area, or smoking it where tobacco smoking is prohibited.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [6] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted No driving under the influence

The adult-use section of the constitution does not allow operating a vehicle under the influence of marijuana, consuming it while operating one, or smoking it in a vehicle that is being operated. A conviction requires evidence that the driver was in fact under the influence, not just THC or its metabolites in the system.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2 · checked November 15, 2026 [6] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Medical cardholders only, with job-safety exceptions

Article XIV, section 1 of the Missouri Constitution bars employers from discriminating in hiring, firing or terms of employment against a qualifying patient or caregiver with a valid card, including for legal use off the employer’s premises outside work hours, or for a positive test, unless the person used, possessed or was under the influence at work or during work hours. It does not apply where acting otherwise would cost the employer a federal monetary or licensing benefit, or to jobs where use affects job performance or others’ safety or conflicts with a bona fide occupational qualification. The adult-use section (section 2) gives employees and applicants no protection.

Mo. Const. art. XIV, § 1, subd. 7(15); art. XIV, § 2 · checked November 23, 2026 [8][6] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Courts expunge past marijuana offenses without a petition

Missouri’s constitution, as amended in 2022, directs the circuit courts to order expungement of misdemeanor marijuana offenses within six months of the amendment taking effect (for people no longer incarcerated or supervised) and of felony marijuana offenses within twelve months (for people who have completed their sentence), with no petition; some higher-level felonies are expunged once incarceration and supervision end (Article XIV, Section 2.10(8), adopted November 8, 2022, effective December 8, 2022). Selling or giving marijuana to a minor, violent offenses and driving under the influence are excluded. A person still incarcerated for an eligible offense may petition the sentencing court to vacate the sentence and expunge the record, with no filing fee. The Missouri courts also provide a petition form for marijuana-related offenses (CR375). The Department of Health and Senior Services says sales-tax revenue pays the government bodies that carry out the expungements.

Missouri Constitution, Article XIV, Section 2.10; Missouri Courts, Marijuana Expungement; Department of Health and Senior Services, Adult use FAQs · checked November 23, 2026 [6][9][4] Official source
