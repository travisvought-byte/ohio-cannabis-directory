Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Allowed Yes, 21 and older

The state’s adult-use page says residents over 21 can legally possess and consume cannabis, and that possessing 1.5 ounces is legal.

Can adults share or gift it?

Allowed Free gifts in a bona fide social relationship

People with a bona fide social relationship may give each other cannabis without payment or exchange and not tied to a sale. Gifts tied to donations, paid-entry locations or event giveaways are prohibited, and medical marijuana may not be gifted.

Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, Can I gift cannabis to other individuals? · checked November 15, 2026 [2] Official source

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Allowed 3 mature and 3 immature, 12 per home

Adults 21 and older may grow up to three mature and three immature plants at their primary residence, with a cap of 12 plants per household. Plants must be indoors, not visible from the street and out of reach of anyone under 21.

Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, Can I grow cannabis at home? · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Varies locally Local rules; banned in state parks

You generally cannot smoke or vape cannabis anywhere tobacco smoking or vaping is banned. Cities and towns may regulate public consumption, and use is prohibited in state parks, on state beaches and on state waters.

Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, Where can I use cannabis? · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted Impaired driving is a DUI

Driving under the influence of cannabis is a criminal offense with the same penalties as an alcohol DUI. Connecticut sets no THC blood level; trained officers assess impairment.

Connecticut Department of Transportation Highway Safety Office, Driving high · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Partly protected Unless an employer policy bars it

Since July 1, 2022, an employer that is not exempt may not prohibit cannabis use outside work, or act on a positive THC test, unless it has adopted a policy that says otherwise. Exempt employers may regulate off-duty use as they could before the law. Employers generally may not act on cannabis use from before a person applied or was hired. The state’s cannabis site says an employer cannot require a medical marijuana patient to refrain from off-duty use.

State of Connecticut, Adult-Use Cannabis in Connecticut, workplace questions (2021 and 2023) · in effect July 1, 2022 · checked November 15, 2026 [6][7] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

Automatic Some possession convictions; others by petition

Connecticut’s Clean Slate site says convictions under General Statutes § 21a-279(c) imposed from January 1, 2000 through September 30, 2015 were set to be erased automatically on January 1, 2023, with nothing for the person to do. Others need a petition to the court, accepted since July 1, 2022: possession of four ounces or less under § 21a-279 imposed before January 1, 2000 or from October 1, 2015 through June 30, 2021; cannabis paraphernalia under § 21a-267(a) imposed before July 1, 2021; and making, selling or giving four ounces or less, or six plants grown at home for personal use, under § 21a-277(b) imposed before July 1, 2021. There is no official petition form; the court either erases the conviction without a hearing or schedules one.

Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 54-142d and 54-142v, per the State of Connecticut’s Clean Slate pages on cannabis erasure · checked November 15, 2026 [8][9] Official source
