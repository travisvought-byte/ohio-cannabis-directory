Cannabis laws in the United Kingdom

Planning a trip to the United Kingdom, or live there? Adults: No. Cannabis is a Class B drug · Buying: No legal retail · Carrying: Warning or £90 on-the-spot fine for small amounts.

Illegal for recreational use. Cannabis is a Class B drug. Specialist doctors have been able to prescribe cannabis-based medicines since November 1, 2018, mostly through private clinics.

What is different about the United Kingdom

  • Recreational use is a crime, yet medical cannabis has been legal since 2018. One 2026 estimate puts UK patients at about 80,000 to 90,000, most of them through private clinics rather than the NHS.
  • Prescribed flower has to be vaporised. Smoking it is a crime even with a prescription.
  • Scotland handles small possession through Recorded Police Warnings set by the Lord Advocate; England and Wales use warnings and £90 fines.
  • The drug-driving limit for THC is 2 µg/L of blood, among the lowest anywhere.
  • Old convictions are never erased. They become spent and disappear from basic checks only.

[1][2][3][4][5][6]

Travelling? Never carry cannabis across an international border, even between two places where it is legal. See where it can land you in prison.

The law in the United Kingdom

Can adults use it without a prescription?

Not permitted No. Cannabis is a Class B drug

Possession carries up to 5 years in prison, an unlimited fine, or both. Supply and production carry up to 14 years. [1]

GOV.UK, Drugs penalties · checked November 19, 2026

Is there a medical program?

Limited Specialist prescription only, never smoked

Since November 1, 2018, cannabis-based products for medicinal use can be prescribed in England, Wales and Scotland, but only by doctors on the General Medical Council's Specialist Register. Smoking them stays illegal, even with a prescription. GPs cannot start a prescription. Since January 2026, national police guidance approved by the National Police Chiefs' Council tells officers in England and Wales how to deal with prescribed patients. [2][7][8][9]

Home Office Circular 018/2018; Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001 reg. 16A; APCDLO guidance; Chambers 2026 (secondary) · checked November 19, 2026

Where can adults buy it legally?

Not permitted No legal retail

There is no legal sale outside a prescription. [1]

GOV.UK, Drugs penalties · checked November 19, 2026

How much can an adult carry?

Not permitted Warning or £90 on-the-spot fine for small amounts

Police in England and Wales can give a warning or an on-the-spot fine of up to £90 for cannabis; repeat offences can go up to the maximum. In Scotland, police can issue a Recorded Police Warning for possession instead of reporting it to the prosecutor; the warning still goes on the person's criminal record. [1][4][10]

GOV.UK, Drugs penalties; Crown Office (COPFS); Police Scotland via STV News · checked November 19, 2026

Can adults grow it at home?

Not permitted No. Growing counts as production

Growing cannabis is production under the Misuse of Drugs Act, with a maximum of 14 years in prison. [1]

GOV.UK, Drugs penalties · checked November 19, 2026

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted 2 micrograms of THC per litre of blood

It is an offence to drive with more than 2 µg/L of THC in your blood, whether or not your driving was affected. The limit is set low on a zero-tolerance approach. [5]

GOV.UK, Changes to drug driving law; Road Traffic Act 1988 s. 5A · checked November 19, 2026

Can an old cannabis record be cleared?

Limited No expungement; records become spent

There is no process to erase a cannabis conviction. Under the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974, a simple caution is spent immediately and a conviction becomes spent after its rehabilitation period. Spent records no longer show on a basic DBS check, but can still show on standard or enhanced checks. [6][11]

GOV.UK, Rehabilitation periods; Disclosing your criminal record · checked November 19, 2026

Good to know

CBD is legal to sell as food only under Food Standards Agency rules. The FSA advises healthy adults to take no more than 10 mg of CBD a day, has set a safe upper limit of 0.07 mg of THC a day, and in September 2026 recommended the first CBD food authorisations to ministers. All CBD products must still comply with the Misuse of Drugs Act. [12][13][14]
