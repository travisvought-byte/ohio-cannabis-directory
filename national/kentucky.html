Is weed legal in Kentucky?

Short answer: Only with a medical card. Recreational weed is not legal in Kentucky, but medical cannabis is.

Checked November 15, 2026 against KRS 218A.1422 [1]

In Kentucky, only registered patients can buy and use cannabis, so for most people the first step is a medical card. Who qualifies, the steps, what it costs and where patients buy, as the state regulator (Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis) gives them, are just below, followed by the list of dispensaries the Office of Medical Cannabis lists as open. How patients get a medical card in Kentucky

From Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis. Adults without a card cannot buy here.

Since July 1, 2026, Kentuckians who buy medical cannabis in another state no longer qualify for the governor’s conditional pardon. [2]

Steps

  1. Get a written certification from a medical cannabis practitioner (the state’s Find a Practitioner directory lists them).
  2. Apply in the Patient and Caregiver Registry Portal with a notarized signature page and the fee.
  3. Buy at an open licensed dispensary.

Steps as the state describes them [3]

Who can get one
Patients with a qualifying condition and a written certification from a medical cannabis practitioner. [3]
Cost
$25. [3]
Caregivers
Allowed. Patients under 18 may use medical cannabis only with a parent or legal guardian as their caregiver. [2]
Visiting patients
Visitors 21 or older with a valid out-of-state card and a documented qualifying condition can register for a Kentucky card. [3]
Where patients buy
Licensed dispensaries. The Office has issued 48 dispensary licenses; it lists the ones that have opened. [2]

Not stated on the state pages we checked: how long it lasts. Check with the state.

The law in Kentucky

The same 10 questions are asked of every state. Each answer names its source and the date it was checked.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Medical patients only No

Kentucky has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor with no more than 45 days in jail. The offense does not apply to medical cannabis cardholders.

KRS 218A.1422 · checked November 15, 2026 [1] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes, since 2025

Senate Bill 47 legalized medical cannabis effective January 1, 2025. The Office of Medical Cannabis runs the program.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis · in effect January 1, 2025 · checked November 15, 2026 [5] Official source

Buying and having it

Where can adults buy it legally?

Medical patients only Cardholders only, at licensed dispensaries

Medical cannabis is sold only at dispensaries licensed by the Office of Medical Cannabis. Cardholders who buy from an out-of-state dispensary may be subject to law enforcement action.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis, General Questions · checked November 17, 2026 [6] Official source

How much can an adult have?

Not yet reviewed No reviewed source yet. This does not mean it is prohibited.

Can adults share or gift it?

Not yet reviewed No reviewed source yet. This does not mean it is prohibited.

Growing at home

Can adults grow it at home?

Not permitted No

Kentucky law prohibits individuals from growing their own medical cannabis without a cannabis business license.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis, General Questions · checked November 17, 2026 [6] Official source

Using it

Can you use it in public?

Limited No smoking; barred at schools, jails, federal property

Cardholders may not consume medical cannabis by smoking. They may not possess or use it on school property unless the school’s policy allows it, at correctional facilities or on federal property. The page does not state a general rule for other public places.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis, General Questions · checked November 17, 2026 [6] Official source

What are the driving rules?

Not permitted Not while under the influence

Medical cannabis cardholders may not operate vehicles or machinery while under the influence of medical cannabis, or keep it within arm’s reach when operating machinery.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis, General Questions · checked November 17, 2026 [6] Official source

Work and past records

Does state law protect workers who use cannabis off the job?

Labels sum up what the cited state law says about employers and cannabis use away from work. They are not a ruling on any one job, employer or drug test.

Not protected Employers may restrict use, test and fire

Kentucky’s medical cannabis law (in effect January 1, 2025) says nothing in it requires an employer to permit or accommodate medical cannabis at work, stops employers from restricting employees’ use or enforcing drug-testing and zero-tolerance policies, or allows a wrongful-discharge or discrimination suit. The Office of Medical Cannabis says workers can be dismissed for on-site use if the employer does not allow it. A separate section gives patients the same rights as prescription-drug users, but only for drug testing that state or local law requires.

KRS 218B.040; KRS 218B.045(1); Office of Medical Cannabis, patient and caregiver FAQs · in effect January 1, 2025 · checked November 23, 2026 [7][8][9] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Court petition with a $40 eligibility certificate

Kentucky’s courts expunge convictions only on petition, filed with the circuit court clerk in the county of the charge with a $40 eligibility certification from the Administrative Office of the Courts. Most Class D felonies are eligible (KRS 431.073), and the courts’ list includes marijuana cultivation under KRS 218A.1423; the wait is five years after the sentence, probation or parole ends, with a $50 filing fee and a $250 fee if granted. Misdemeanor and violation convictions go through KRS 431.078, whose waiting period and fee the page does not state. Cases dismissed with prejudice or ending in acquittal on or after July 15, 2020, other than traffic cases, are expunged automatically after 30 days unless the defendant objects. The law covers criminal records in general.

KRS 431.073, 431.076 and 431.078, per the Kentucky Court of Justice’s Expungement Certification Process page · checked November 16, 2026 [10] Official source
