How patients get a medical card in Kentucky

From Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis. Adults without a card cannot buy here.

Since July 1, 2026, Kentuckians who buy medical cannabis in another state no longer qualify for the governor’s conditional pardon. [1]

Steps

  1. Get a written certification from a medical cannabis practitioner (the state’s Find a Practitioner directory lists them).
  2. Apply in the Patient and Caregiver Registry Portal with a notarized signature page and the fee.
  3. Buy at an open licensed dispensary.

Steps as the state describes them [2]

Who can get one
Patients with a qualifying condition and a written certification from a medical cannabis practitioner. [2]
Cost
$25. [2]
Caregivers
Allowed. Patients under 18 may use medical cannabis only with a parent or legal guardian as their caregiver. [1]
Visiting patients
Visitors 21 or older with a valid out-of-state card and a documented qualifying condition can register for a Kentucky card. [2]
Where patients buy
Licensed dispensaries. The Office has issued 48 dispensary licenses; it lists the ones that have opened. [1]

Not stated on the state pages we checked: how long it lasts. Check with the state.

Is it legal here?

Can adults 21 and older use cannabis without a medical card?

Medical patients only No

Kentucky has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor with no more than 45 days in jail. The offense does not apply to medical cannabis cardholders.

KRS 218A.1422 · checked November 15, 2026 [3] Official source

Is there a state medical cannabis program?

Allowed Yes, since 2025

Senate Bill 47 legalized medical cannabis effective January 1, 2025. The Office of Medical Cannabis runs the program.

Kentucky Office of Medical Cannabis · in effect January 1, 2025 · checked November 15, 2026 [4] Official source

Can a past cannabis record be cleared?

Labels name the process the cited state law provides. Whether one record qualifies depends on that record.

By application Court petition with a $40 eligibility certificate

Kentucky’s courts expunge convictions only on petition, filed with the circuit court clerk in the county of the charge with a $40 eligibility certification from the Administrative Office of the Courts. Most Class D felonies are eligible (KRS 431.073), and the courts’ list includes marijuana cultivation under KRS 218A.1423; the wait is five years after the sentence, probation or parole ends, with a $50 filing fee and a $250 fee if granted. Misdemeanor and violation convictions go through KRS 431.078, whose waiting period and fee the page does not state. Cases dismissed with prejudice or ending in acquittal on or after July 15, 2020, other than traffic cases, are expunged automatically after 30 days unless the defendant objects. The law covers criminal records in general.

KRS 431.073, 431.076 and 431.078, per the Kentucky Court of Justice’s Expungement Certification Process page · checked November 16, 2026 [5] Official source
