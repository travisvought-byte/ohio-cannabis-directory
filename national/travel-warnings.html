Where weed can put you in prison

Flying abroad or driving through a strict state? Read this before you pack. Legal at home does not mean legal where you land, and some countries treat any amount, or cannabis still in your bloodstream, as a serious crime.

The short version: don't fly with it, don't cross a border with it, and check whether a country tests travellers before you go. That includes CBD and vape cartridges.

Countries with the harshest penalties

Singapore Death penalty possible

Possession of even very small quantities can lead to the death penalty, according to UK travel advice. Singapore classes cannabis as a Class A drug, with up to 10 years in prison and a fine of up to S$20,000 for consumption. Drug use detected on arrival can mean arrest. [1][2][3]

Checked November 19, 2026

Indonesia Death penalty possible

Zero tolerance. Possessing, using, making or trafficking drugs can bring long prison sentences or the death penalty. Airports scan transit passengers' bags for cannabis. [4]

Checked November 19, 2026

Malaysia Long prison terms

Possession, trafficking and manufacture of any illegal drug, including cannabis, are serious offences. British travellers have been caught carrying cannabis while only transiting. Australia lists Malaysia among countries that apply the death penalty to drug offences. [5][6]

Checked November 19, 2026

Mexico Medical cards do not count

Possession is a crime outside a COFEPRIS permit, and visitors have no permit. The U.S. government warns that drug possession and use, including medical marijuana, is illegal in Mexico and may result in a lengthy jail sentence. [7]

Checked November 19, 2026

United Arab Emirates Cannabis in your blood counts

UAE authorities treat drugs found in your bloodstream as possession, so use before arrival can lead to charges. Some CBD products are illegal. [8]

Checked November 19, 2026

Japan Up to 7 years for use

Since December 12, 2024, using cannabis is a crime. Possession, use and transfer each carry up to 7 years in prison, and import carries 1 to 10 years. [9][10]

Checked November 19, 2026

South Korea Up to 5 years

Possession or use can bring up to 5 years in prison, and smuggling carries much longer terms, followed by deportation and an entry ban (Thai embassy warning). [11]

Checked November 19, 2026

Thailand Up to 10 years at the border

Since June 2026, bringing cannabis across Thailand's border can mean up to 10 years in prison, a fine of up to 500,000 baht, or both. [12]

Checked November 19, 2026

China, Vietnam, Sri Lanka Death penalty possible

Australia's government lists these among countries that apply the death penalty to drug offences. [6]

Checked November 19, 2026

The strictest US states

These 11 states have no adult-use law and no full medical program, and some penalties reach felony level. Each answer comes from the state page and links to its source.

Texas: vapes and edibles are felonies. In Texas, THC concentrates (vape oil, wax, most edibles) are not treated as marijuana. They are Penalty Group 2 controlled substances, and possessing less than 1 gram is a state jail felony: 180 days to 2 years and a fine of up to $10,000. [44][45]
Tex. Health & Safety Code 481.103 and 481.116; NORML penalty table
  • GeorgiaGeorgia has no adult-use law. The state’s Access to Medical Cannabis Commission says adult-use marijuana is not legal and that growing, possessing and smoking marijuana are crimes. Possessing 1 ounce or less is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 12 months in jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both. [13][14]

    Medical: Limited Low-THC oil program only · checked November 15, 2026

  • IdahoIdaho has no adult-use law. Possessing 3 ounces of marijuana or less is a misdemeanor, with a fine of at least $300 for anyone 18 or older. More than 3 ounces is a felony punishable by up to 5 years in prison, a fine of up to $10,000, or both. [15]

    Medical: Not permitted No medical marijuana law · checked November 15, 2026

  • IndianaIndiana has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor. It is a Class A misdemeanor with a prior drug conviction, and a Level 6 felony with a prior drug conviction and 30 grams or more. [16]

    Medical: Not permitted No medical marijuana law · checked November 15, 2026

  • IowaIowa has no adult-use law. A first conviction for possessing marijuana can bring up to six months in jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both. Penalties rise for repeat offenses. [17]

    Medical: Limited Medical cannabidiol card program · checked November 15, 2026

  • KansasKansas has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a Class B nonperson misdemeanor. One prior conviction makes it a Class A misdemeanor, and two or more make it a drug felony. [18]

    Medical: Limited Legal defense for CBD treatment oil · checked November 15, 2026

  • North CarolinaNorth Carolina has no adult-use law. Possessing half an ounce or less is a Class 3 misdemeanor. More than half an ounce, up to 1.5 ounces, is a Class 1 misdemeanor, and more than 1.5 ounces is a Class I felony. [19]

    Medical: Limited Hemp extract for intractable epilepsy · checked November 15, 2026

  • South CarolinaSouth Carolina has no adult-use law. A first offense for 1 ounce or less is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 30 days in jail or a fine of $100 to $200. A second or later offense can bring up to a year in jail or a fine of at least $200. [20]

    Medical: Limited Low-THC CBD for severe epilepsy · checked November 15, 2026

  • TennesseeTennessee has no adult-use law. The Attorney General’s 2016 opinion describes possessing any amount as a Class A misdemeanor (up to 11 months and 29 days in jail, a fine of up to $2,500, or both), with minimum fines starting at $250. National compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [21][22][23]

    Medical: Limited CBD oil only · checked November 15, 2026

  • TexasTexas has no adult-use law. Possessing under 2 ounces of marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor, punishable by jail time and fines. That is how the Senate Research Center described state law in March 2025; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [24][22][23]

    Medical: Limited Low-THC program only · checked November 15, 2026

  • WisconsinWisconsin has no adult-use law. A first conviction for possession is a misdemeanor (a fine of up to $1,000, up to six months in jail, or both), and a repeat conviction is a Class I felony. That is the Legislative Reference Bureau’s December 2023 summary of current law; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [25][22][23]

    Medical: Limited CBD with a physician’s certification · checked November 15, 2026

  • WyomingWyoming has no adult-use law. Possessing up to 3 ounces of marijuana in plant form is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 12 months in jail, a $1,000 fine, or both. Larger amounts are a felony punishable by up to 5 years in prison, a $10,000 fine, or both. [26]

    Medical: Not permitted No medical marijuana law · checked November 15, 2026

Medical card only

In these 15 states, cannabis is legal only for registered patients. Without a card, the state's criminal penalties apply.

  • AlabamaAlabama has no adult-use law. A first offense of possessing marijuana for personal use is a Class A misdemeanor. That is existing law as summarized in a February 2026 Senate bill; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [27][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • ArkansasArkansas has no adult-use law. Possessing less than 4 ounces of marijuana is a Class A misdemeanor. That is the law as the Arkansas Sentencing Commission quoted it in 2019; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [28][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • FloridaFlorida has no adult-use law. Possessing 20 grams of cannabis or less is a first-degree misdemeanor; larger amounts are felonies. [29]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • HawaiiHawaii has no adult-use law. Possessing 3 grams of marijuana or less is a violation with a $130 fine rather than a crime, and distribution remains illegal. That is how the Attorney General’s office described current law in February 2024; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [30][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • KentuckyKentucky has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor with no more than 45 days in jail. The offense does not apply to medical cannabis cardholders. [31]

    Medical: Allowed Yes, since 2025 · checked November 15, 2026

  • LouisianaLouisiana has no adult-use law. Without medical authorization, possessing 14 grams or less brings a summons and a fine of up to $100. A first conviction for more than 14 grams can bring up to six months in jail, a $500 fine, or both. [32]

    Medical: Allowed Yes, with a clinician’s recommendation · checked November 15, 2026

  • MississippiMississippi has no adult-use law. The state Health Department’s medical cannabis program says recreational use and possession remain illegal. Its patient toolkit lists a $250 fine for a first offense of up to 30 grams outside the program, jail time for repeat offenses, and a felony for more than 30 grams. [33]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • NebraskaNebraska has no adult-use law. Without a medical recommendation, a first offense for 1 ounce or less is an infraction with a citation and a $300 fine, and a drug education course may be required. More than 1 ounce, up to 1 pound, is a Class III misdemeanor. [34]

    Medical: Allowed Yes, with a written recommendation · checked November 15, 2026

  • New HampshireNew Hampshire has no adult-use law. Since 2017, possessing three-quarters of an ounce of marijuana or less has been a violation, not a crime, for people 18 and older; possessing more is a misdemeanor. That is how the Attorney General’s office described the law in a 2019 court brief; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [35][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • North DakotaNorth Dakota has no adult-use law. Without a medical card, possessing less than half an ounce is an infraction. Half an ounce up to 500 grams is a class B misdemeanor, and more than 500 grams is a class A misdemeanor. [36]

    Medical: Allowed Yes, with a state registry card · checked November 15, 2026

  • OklahomaOklahoma has no adult-use law. In March 2023, more than 61% of voters rejected State Question 820, which would have legalized recreational marijuana. National compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [37][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • PennsylvaniaPennsylvania has no adult-use law. Without a medical card, possessing up to 30 grams of marijuana or 8 grams of hashish is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 30 days in jail, a fine of up to $500, or both. [38]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked October 28, 2026

  • South DakotaSouth Dakota has no adult-use law. The state Health Department says any sale or purchase of cannabis outside licensed medical cannabis establishments is illegal. Possessing 2 ounces or less is a Class 1 misdemeanor (up to one year in county jail, a fine of up to $2,000, or both), as Legislative Research Council staff summarized the law in 2021; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [39][40][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • UtahUtah has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a class B misdemeanor, and a third conviction within seven years is a class A misdemeanor. That is the law as reprinted in a January 2026 House bill; national compilations list no adult use as of November 2026. [41][22][23]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026

  • West VirginiaWest Virginia has no adult-use law. Possessing marijuana is a misdemeanor punishable by 90 days to 6 months in jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both. A first offense involving less than 15 grams must be handled through conditional discharge, under which completing probation ends the case without a conviction. [42][43]

    Medical: Allowed Yes · checked November 15, 2026
