Pennsylvania cannabis policy: find the decision point

Pennsylvania has a regulated medical marijuana program. Adult-use legalization has not become law. This pilot starts with the current bill record, the officials who can act, and a short list of questions residents can raise.

Find your Pennsylvania legislatorRead HB 1200’s official record

Where the proposal stands

House Bill 1200, sponsored by Representative Rick Krajewski, would legalize and regulate adult-use cannabis through a Pennsylvania Cannabis Stores model and includes expungement, equity and revenue provisions. The House passed it 102–101 on May 7, 2025. The Senate referred it to Law & Justice; a motion to report the bill was defeated 3–7 on May 13, 2025. The official bill page is the source of truth for later actions.

That vote describes this specific bill and model. It does not mean every adult-use proposal has been rejected or that no further action can occur.

Who can move the issue

Your state representative and senator

Ask what they support, which bill or amendment they would advance, and what changes they need to see. Contact details and district assignments are available through the official General Assembly portal.

House and Senate committees

Committee referrals and meetings determine whether proposals receive hearings and votes. HB 1200’s Senate referral was to Law & Justice. Follow its official bill history and committee calendar for changes.

Bill sponsors

Rep. Rick Krajewski is the prime sponsor of HB 1200. Sen. Daniel Laughlin and Sen. Sharif Street sponsored the earlier bipartisan SB 846 in the 2023–2024 session; that bill is historical, not a current-session vehicle.

Medical program administrator

The Pennsylvania Department of Health administers the existing medical marijuana program. It is not an adult-use regulator under current law.

Useful questions to raise

  • Market structure: What model can pass and still provide a workable route for independent Pennsylvania businesses, patients, workers and local communities?
  • Equity and records: What automatic record-clearing, licensing access and reinvestment provisions should accompany legalization?
  • Medical patients: How should adult-use rules protect patient access, affordability and continuity of care?
  • Local impact: How would tax revenue, local authority, public health protections and community costs be measured and reported?

When comparing proposals from Ohio or other states, treat their results as evidence to examine—not as a guarantee Pennsylvania would get the same outcome. Ask which conditions differ, who benefits, and what safeguards are included.

Take one practical step

  1. Use the official portal to identify your state representative and senator.
  2. Open the current HB 1200 record and check its status before referring to it.
  3. Send a short message asking your officials what specific action they support on adult-use legislation and what would address their concerns.
  4. Follow committee meetings and bill calendars for the next opportunity to provide input.

Message starter: “I live in [town or district]. I’m asking you to explain what specific adult-use cannabis legislation or amendments you would support, including the market structure, patient protections, record clearing, equity, and local revenue safeguards. What action will you take, and how can I follow it?”
